Cost To Paint Interior Of House In Miami 2026

Updated May 22, 2026 11 min read
Written by Marco Reyes, Florida Home Improvement Specialist

How Much Does It Cost to Paint Interior of House in Miami? (2026 Guide)

Quick Answer: A full interior paint job in Miami costs between $3,500 and $15,000 for most single-family homes, with the average homeowner paying around $7,500. The biggest factors driving your price are square footage, surface preparation needs, and the premium labor rates in South Florida.

Miami homes face unique challenges that standard paint jobs elsewhere do not. High humidity, salt air, and frequent rain cycles mean you need mold-resistant products and specific prep work that keeps paint from peeling.

Budget covers painting one or two rooms in a small condo or townhome. It uses builder-grade paint, minimal surface repair, and standard prep work. It meets basic aesthetic needs but may not handle Miami humidity long-term.

High-end includes custom color matching, trim and cabinetry painting, extensive drywall repair, and premium low-VOC paints. This scope often involves two-story homes with high ceilings or historic properties requiring careful preservation.

These prices reflect Miami market rates as of early 2026. They include materials, labor, masking, drop cloths, and cleanup. They do not include major structural repairs or mold remediation if found behind walls.

What Affects the Cost in Miami

1. Square Footage

Painting is priced by the square footage of wall surface, not floor space. A typical 2,000 sq ft Miami home has roughly 4,000 to 5,000 sq ft of wall surface. At $2.50–$4.50 per sq ft of wall surface, a standard job runs $10,000–$13,000. Smaller condos might fall into the $3,500–$5,000 range.

2. Surface Condition

Miami homes often have moisture issues due to high humidity. If walls have water stains, mold, or bubbling paint, you need remediation before painting. Minor patching adds $500–$1,000. Major drywall replacement or mold remediation can add $2,000–$5,000 to the project.

3. Paint Quality

Paint costs vary wildly. Builder-grade paint costs $25–$40 per gallon but may need two coats. Premium paints like Sherwin Williams or Benjamin Moore cost $60–$90 per gallon and often require only one coat. Upgrading paint quality adds $500–$1,500 to the total project cost.

4. Labor Rates in Miami

Labor in Miami-Dade runs $45–$70 per hour per painter. This is 20–30% higher than the national average due to demand and cost of living. A full interior paint job typically takes 5–10 days for a crew of two. High demand during dry season pushes rates up.

5. Trim and Cabinets

Baseboards, door frames, and window casings require detailed brush and roller work. Painting trim adds $1.50–$3.00 per linear foot. Kitchen cabinets require sanding, deglossing, and multiple coats for durability. Cabinet painting adds $1,500–$4,000 depending on the number of cabinets.

6. Ceiling Height

Standard Miami homes have 8-foot ceilings. Luxury homes and older estates often feature 10-foot or 12-foot ceilings. Working at height requires scaffolding or extension poles. High ceilings can increase labor time by 20–30%, adding $1,000–$2,500 to the quote.

Walls represent the bulk of the cost. This includes sanding, priming, and two coats of finish paint. Walls in bathrooms and kitchens require moisture-resistant paint, which costs slightly more but prevents peeling in humid conditions.

Ceilings are usually painted white or off-white. If you want a colored ceiling or texture work, expect to pay higher. Textured ceilings require more paint volume and careful rolling techniques to avoid splatter.

Trim work involves intricate cutting around corners and edges. It requires patience and skill. If you have ornate crown molding common in Coral Gables or Miami Beach, labor costs increase due to the detail work required.

Cabinets are high-traffic surfaces. They need a durable finish that can handle cleaning and moisture. Spray application provides the smoothest finish but costs more than brushing. Expect $40–$80 per door for a full kitchen transformation.

Doors include both the slab and the frame. Painting both sides ensures a finished look. Interior doors usually need at least two coats for even coverage. Solid wood doors absorb more paint than hollow core doors.

Stairs involve treads, risers, and handrails. Handrails require sanding and multiple thin coats to avoid drips. If you have wrought iron railings, they need a different primer and finish than wood.

Florida-Specific Considerations

Building Codes: Miami-Dade County follows the Florida Building Code, which has strict moisture control requirements. While interior paint doesn’t usually require a building permit, you must use low-VOC paints in some jurisdictions to meet indoor air quality standards. Check local ordinances before starting.

Humidity and Mold: Miami humidity averages 70–80% year-round. Standard paint can peel or bubble within months if the substrate isn’t sealed properly. Use mildew-resistant primer and paint in all bathrooms and kitchens. This adds $200–$500 but saves replacement costs later.

Permits: Interior painting rarely requires a permit in Miami-Dade unless you are changing wall configurations or working in a historic district. If your home is in the Miami Beach Historic District, you may need approval for color changes even inside. Budget time for 3–5 days if review is needed.

Insurance Impact: Poorly painted walls that trap moisture can lead to mold claims. A new paint job with proper moisture barriers can prevent future claims. Some insurers offer discounts for homes with documented mold prevention measures during the inspection process.

HOA: Many Miami communities have strict aesthetic rules. Even interior work visible through windows or in common areas might be regulated. Get HOA approval in writing before ordering materials. Violating covenants can force you to repaint at your own expense.

Season: Best time to paint is December through April. Humidity is lower, allowing paint to cure faster. Avoid painting during hurricane season (June–November) when high moisture can trap water vapor in walls. Rain delays can extend project timelines by 1–2 weeks.

My Safe Florida Home: This program offers free inspections and grants for wind mitigation. While it doesn’t fund painting, the inspection often identifies moisture issues. Use the inspection report to prioritize repairs before painting. Homeowners with grants up to $10,000 may use funds for necessary prep work.

How to Save Money on Interior Painting in Miami

  1. Move your own furniture. Contractors charge $500–$1,500 to move and protect furniture. Moving it yourself and clearing the room saves significant labor time. Just make sure to protect floors yourself before they arrive.

  2. Choose standard colors. Custom color matching adds $100–$300 per color. White, off-white, and gray are standard mixes. Ask your contractor to stick to stock colors available at local paint stores to avoid delays and extra charges.

  3. Paint during the dry season. Scheduling in January or February often yields better pricing. Contractors are less busy than during storm repair season. You might negotiate a 5–10% discount for flexible scheduling.

  4. Buy the paint yourself. Contractors mark up paint by 20–30%. If you buy premium paint yourself, you save money. Ensure the contractor agrees to this arrangement in the contract so they don’t void their warranty on application.

  5. Limit scope to high-impact areas. Painting only the main living areas, kitchen, and master bedroom saves thousands. Leave guest rooms and closets for later. This prioritizes your budget while maximizing resale value impact.

  6. Fix walls yourself. Patch small nail holes and cracks before the painters arrive. This reduces their prep time. It saves roughly $200–$500 if you handle minor cosmetic fixes beforehand.

  7. Get multiple bids. Miami contractor pricing varies by 25–40%. On a $7,000 job, the difference between the cheapest and most expensive quote could be $2,000. Don’t just pick the lowest bid — check references.

When to Paint Your Home

You are selling the house. Fresh paint increases resale value. Buyers in Miami respond well to neutral tones that make spaces look clean and dry. A fresh coat can recoup 80–100% of its cost at closing, especially in a competitive market.

You see mold spots. Dark corners in bathrooms or behind furniture often harbor mold. Painting over mold without killing it causes it to bleed through. Treat the area first, then paint. Ignoring this leads to health issues and costly re-painting later.

Your paint is peeling. Florida heat and humidity cause paint to expand and contract. If you see cracking or peeling, the adhesive has failed. This usually happens after 7–10 years. Address it before the drywall substrate rots.

You changed your style. Neutral walls from 10 years ago look dated. Updating to modern grays or warm whites refreshes the home without a full remodel. This is the most cost-effective way to update a home’s look.

You moved in. New homeowners often repaint to remove previous tenants’ scents and marks. It sets a fresh tone for your family. Budget for at least one coat on all walls and two on trim for a professional finish.

How to Hire a Painter in Miami

  1. Verify their Florida license. Check at MyFloridaLicense.com. You need a Certified General Contractor (CGC) or a Certified Painting Contractor (CPC). Interior work often falls under general contracting if it involves drywall repair.

  2. Confirm active insurance. Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $1 million) and workers comp. Call the insurer to verify. Miami contractors sometimes skip coverage — this leaves you liable for injuries.

  3. Check for local business tax receipt. This confirms they are registered in Miami-Dade County. It prevents hiring out-of-state storm chasers who disappear after the job. You can verify this on the County’s official portal.

  4. Get 3–4 written estimates. Each estimate should list: paint brand, coats, prep work, protection, and timeline. Look for line-item breakdowns. Vague quotes like “paint the house” hide hidden costs later.

  5. Check references from local jobs. Ask for addresses of recent projects in your neighborhood. Drive by to see the finish quality. Paint jobs age visibly in Florida humidity. Old projects tell you more than recent photos.

  6. Never pay more than 30% upfront. Florida Statute 489.126 restricts deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, prep complete, first coat, final inspection. Protect your cash flow until the job is done.

  7. Get warranty in writing. A good painter stands behind their work for 2–5 years. Written warranty covers peeling, blistering, or color fading. Verbal promises are worthless in court if the paint fails in year three.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to paint a 3-bedroom house in Miami?

A 3-bedroom, 2,000 sq ft home typically costs $6,000–$9,000. This includes walls, ceilings, and trim. The price depends on wall condition and paint quality. High ceilings or extensive drywall repair adds to the total.

Do I need a permit to paint the interior in Miami-Dade?

No, painting walls usually does not require a permit. However, if you remove drywall or change electrical boxes, you do. Historic districts may require color approval even for interior visible through windows. Always check with the building department.

How long does an interior paint job take in Miami?

A standard 2,000 sq ft home takes 5–7 days to complete. This includes prep, priming, and two coats of paint. Humidity can extend drying times. Avoid scheduling during the rainy season to prevent delays.

Does painting increase home value in Miami?

Fresh paint increases resale value by 5–10% of the asking price. Neutral colors appeal to the widest pool of buyers. In Miami’s competitive market, a fresh coat makes the home show better and sell faster.

Can I paint over existing dark walls?

Yes, but you need a high-quality primer first. Covering dark colors with light paint requires extra coats. Expect to pay $1.00–$2.00 per sq ft extra for coverage. Skipping primer leads to uneven color and peeling.

What is the best paint for Miami humidity?

Use 100% acrylic latex with mildewcides. Brands like Sherwin Williams Duration or Benjamin Moore Aura offer high durability. Avoid oil-based paints which yellow over time. Ensure the paint is rated for high-moisture areas.

Bottom Line

Painting the interior of a house in Miami costs $3,500 to $15,000 depending on size and finish quality. The average homeowner pays around $7,500 for a full home refresh. The most important factor is choosing paint that handles humidity and mold. Get quotes from at least 3 licensed contractors and verify their insurance status.
