Cost To Clean Air Ducts In Fort Lauderdale 2026

Updated May 15, 2026 9 min read
Written by Marco Reyes, Florida Home Improvement Specialist

How Much Does It Cost to Clean Air Ducts in Fort Lauderdale? (2026 Guide)

Quick Answer: A professional air duct cleaning in Fort Lauderdale costs between $350 and $850 for a standard residential system. Deep cleaning with sanitization or heavy mold remediation prep runs $900 to $1,500. The final price depends on your home size, number of vents, and how contaminated the system actually is.

Fort Lauderdale’s coastal humidity makes duct cleaning more critical here than in drier climates. Moisture builds up inside HVAC systems year-round, creating the perfect environment for mold, mildew, and dust mites.

Average Cost Breakdown

Cost Level Price Range Typical Scope
Budget $350 – $550 3-4 bedrooms, standard system
Mid-Range $550 – $900 5+ vents, light sanitization
High-End $900 – $1,500+ Mold prep, heavy debris

Budget pricing covers standard cleaning for a typical 2,000 sq ft home. It includes agitation of supply and return lines, vacuuming main trunks, and cleaning register covers.

Mid-range adds sanitization treatments to kill bacteria and odor removal services. It also includes cleaning the blower motor and evaporator coil if accessible.

High-end addresses significant contamination like visible mold growth or pest infestation. This often requires specialized equipment and follows EPA guidelines for remediation.

These prices reflect Fort Lauderdale market rates as of early 2026 and include labor, equipment usage, and disposal of debris. They generally do not include repairs to damaged ductwork or new installation of ducts.

What Affects the Cost in Fort Lauderdale

1. Home Size and Square Footage

Duct cleaning is often priced per square foot or by the number of vents. A 1,500 sq ft condo in Fort Lauderdale costs less than a 4,000 sq ft estate in Coral Ridge. Expect to pay $0.15 to $0.35 per square foot for a standard clean.

2. Number of Vents and Registers

Most contractors charge $25 to $50 per vent. A home with 15 vents will cost roughly $400–$750 more than one with 5 vents. This includes cleaning the individual register grilles and the accessible duct branches.

3. System Accessibility

If your air handler is in a cramped attic or crawlspace, labor time increases. Fort Lauderdale has many homes with slab-on-grade construction where ducts run under the foundation. Accessing these lines takes longer and costs $100–$200 extra.

4. Level of Contamination

Standard dust removal is cheaper than heavy debris. If there is rodent droppings, heavy mold spores, or construction dust, the job requires more time and safety gear. This can add $200–$400 to the total bill.

5. Sanitization and Deodorization

Adding antimicrobial coating or ozone treatment increases the cost by $150–$300. This is highly recommended for Fort Lauderdale homes due to the high humidity levels that encourage microbial growth.

6. Seasonal Demand

Prices rise during peak HVAC season (May–October) when AC demand is highest. Scheduling during the cooler months (December–March) can save you 10–15% on labor rates.

Cost by Service Type

Service Type Cost Range Duration
Standard Clean $350 – $550 2–3 hours
Deep Clean $550 – $900 3–5 hours
Sanitization $900 – $1,500 4–6 hours

Standard clean removes visible dust and debris from ducts and registers. It does not include chemical treatments. Best for routine maintenance every 3–5 years.

Deep clean includes agitation of the blower wheel, cleaning the evaporator coil, and vacuuming the main trunk lines more aggressively. Recommended for homes with pets or smokers.

Sanitization adds EPA-registered antimicrobial treatments to the interior of the ductwork. This is crucial for homes with allergy sufferers or a history of moisture issues.

Florida-Specific Considerations

Humidity and Mold: Fort Lauderdale averages 75% humidity year-round. HVAC ducts act as condensers, pulling moisture from the air. This moisture promotes mold growth inside the ductwork faster than in inland areas. The Florida Building Code requires proper drainage for AC systems to mitigate this risk.

Permits: Broward County generally does not require a building permit for duct cleaning alone. However, if you are modifying ductwork or installing new lines, you need a permit. Always confirm with the contractor that no structural changes are being made.

Licensing: Florida requires HVAC contractors to hold a state license. Look for a Certified Air Conditioning Contractor (CAC) or Certified General Contractor (CGC) license. Unlicensed operators often cut corners on equipment and insurance.

Insurance Impact: Some homeowners insurance policies cover mold remediation if it stems from sudden water damage. Regular duct cleaning can help prevent mold claims, but it is not a substitute for fixing leaks. Document your cleaning for insurance records.

Pollen and Allergens: South Florida has high pollen counts year-round, especially from pine and oak trees. Ducts collect this pollen, which then circulates through your home. Cleaning reduces allergens and can lower allergy medication costs.

Hurricane Prep: Before hurricane season (June–November), ensure your ducts are clean. Storm damage can force debris into air intakes. Clean ducts improve airflow efficiency, which helps your AC run cooler during the heat of summer.

My Safe Florida Home: While this program focuses on wind mitigation, improving HVAC efficiency can contribute to overall home hardening. Some rebates cover high-efficiency filters or upgrades that work in tandem with duct cleaning.

How to Save Money on Duct Cleaning in Fort Lauderdale

  1. Bundle with AC Tune-Up. Many HVAC companies offer discounts when you combine duct cleaning with an annual AC maintenance visit. You can save $100–$200 by scheduling both services together.

  2. Schedule in Off-Season. Plan your cleaning between November and March. Contractors have more availability and may offer lower rates to fill their schedules before the summer rush.

  3. Check Your Filters. If you change your HVAC filters every month, your ducts stay cleaner longer. This can extend the time between professional cleanings from 2 years to 4–5 years.

  4. Get Multiple Quotes. Pricing varies significantly between local companies. Get at least 3 written estimates to ensure you are paying a fair market rate. Avoid the cheapest option if it lacks proper insurance.

  5. Ask About Flat Rates. Some contractors charge by the hour, which can spiral if they find delays. Request a flat-rate price for your specific home size to avoid surprise charges.

  6. Clean Vents Yourself. Vacuuming registers yourself before the pro arrives can save time. However, do not attempt to clean the main trunk lines yourself without proper equipment.

  7. Look for Rebates. Check with your utility provider, like Florida Power & Light. Sometimes they offer rebates for air quality improvements or energy efficiency upgrades that include duct sealing.

When to Clean Your Ducts — Warning Signs

You see visible dust blowing out. When you turn on the AC, watch the registers. If dust clouds appear, the ducts are dirty. This indicates a buildup of debris that is circulating into your living space.

Musty odors persist. If your home smells like mold or mildew even when the AC is off, the ducts are likely the source. This is common in Fort Lauderdale due to the humidity.

You have recently renovated. Construction dust from remodeling projects gets into the HVAC system. You should clean the ducts immediately after finishing major home renovations to protect your health.

Pets shed heavily. Cats and dogs shed dander and hair that gets sucked into the return vents. This accumulates quickly in Florida homes. Clean every 2 years if you have multiple pets.

Allergy symptoms worsen. If family members experience more sneezing or coughing when the AC runs, the air quality may be poor. Duct cleaning can significantly improve indoor air quality for allergy sufferers.

How to Hire a Duct Cleaning Contractor in Fort Lauderdale

  1. Verify Florida State License. Check the license at MyFloridaLicense.com. You need a Certified Air Conditioning Contractor (CAC) or Certified General Contractor (CGC).

  2. Check NADCA Certification. The National Air Duct Cleaners Association sets industry standards. Look for the NADCA certification logo on their website or truck.

  3. Confirm Insurance Coverage. Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability and workers comp. You do not want to be liable if a worker gets hurt in your home.

  4. Ask About Equipment. They should use truck-mounted vacuum units, not portable shop vacs. Truck-mounted units have much stronger suction and better filtration capabilities.

  5. Get a Detailed Scope of Work. The quote should list every vent, the main trunk, blower motor, and coil cleaning. Vague quotes often lead to hidden fees later.

  6. Review the Warranty. A reputable company offers a warranty on their work, typically 1–2 years. This covers any recontamination due to their cleaning method.

  7. Avoid Up-Selling on Sealing. Some companies push duct sealing at $2,000+. Only pay for sealing if a professional inspection confirms significant leaks in your ductwork.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does duct cleaning cost in Fort Lauderdale in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between $350 and $850 for a standard cleaning. Deep cleaning with sanitization can cost $900 to $1,500. Prices depend on home size and the number of vents.

Do I need a permit for duct cleaning in Broward County?

No, a building permit is not required for cleaning alone. However, if the contractor modifies or adds ductwork, a permit is necessary. Verify this with your contractor before starting.

How often should I clean my air ducts?

The EPA recommends cleaning every 3–5 years. However, in Fort Lauderdale’s humid climate, every 2 years is better for homes with pets or allergy sufferers.

Can I clean my own air ducts?

DIY is not recommended. You need specialized agitation tools and high-powered vacuums. Improper cleaning can damage ducts or release more dust into your home than you started with.

Does duct cleaning improve AC efficiency?

Yes, clean ducts improve airflow. This can reduce your energy bill by 5–15% in some cases. It also extends the life of your AC unit by reducing strain on the blower motor.

Is duct cleaning safe for pets and children?

Yes, provided the company uses EPA-registered products. Ask the contractor what chemicals they use before they start. Many use ozone or hydrogen peroxide which are safe once the air clears.

Bottom Line

Cleaning air ducts in Fort Lauderdale costs $350 to $1,500 depending on your home size and contamination level. The high humidity in Broward County makes this service more necessary than in other states. Always hire a licensed HVAC professional with NADCA certification to ensure safety and effectiveness.
