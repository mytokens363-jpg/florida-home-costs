Cost To Build Inground Pool In Fort Lauderdale 2026

Updated May 16, 2026 9 min read
Written by Marco Reyes, Florida Home Improvement Specialist

How Much Does It Cost to Build an Inground Pool in Fort Lauderdale? (2026 Guide)

Quick Answer: Building an inground pool in Fort Lauderdale costs between $65,000 and $180,000 for most residential projects, with the average homeowner spending around $115,000. The biggest factors driving your price are pool size, material choice, soil conditions, and Broward County permit fees.

Fort Lauderdale sits on a high water table with sandy soil that complicates excavation. This adds cost compared to inland Florida markets, but it also means your pool is built to handle the local environment.

Average Cost Breakdown

Cost Level Price Range Typical Scope
Budget $65,000 – $85,000 Vinyl liner, basic shape, no spa
Mid-Range $85,000 – $130,000 Gunite, tile, standard coping

Budget gets you a standard vinyl liner pool or small concrete shell. It meets code but uses entry-level equipment and basic finishes.

Mid-range is what most Fort Lauderdale homeowners choose — gunite construction with pebble finishes, standard automation, and a diving board or ladder. Covers pools up to 12x24 feet.

High-end covers large custom shapes, saltwater systems, automated covers, and integrated spas. Includes upgraded lighting and high-efficiency pumps.

These prices reflect Fort Lauderdale market rates as of early 2026 and include excavation, shell, plumbing, electrical, and standard permits. They do not include landscaping, fencing, or patio pavers.

What Affects the Cost in Fort Lauderdale

1. Excavation and Soil Conditions

Fort Lauderdale soil is sandy with a high water table. You may need dewatering pumps or shoring to keep the hole open. This adds $5,000–$15,000 to the base cost. If you hit bedrock, which is rare but possible in parts of Fort Lauderdale, costs spike significantly.

2. Pool Size and Shape

A standard rectangle is the most efficient to build. Add curves, free-form edges, or tanning ledges and labor climbs. Complex shapes can add $10,000–$25,000 to the project. A larger surface area requires more gunite and plumbing.

3. Material Choice

This is the single biggest price lever you control. Concrete (gunite) is the most common at $65–$85 per sq ft installed. Fiberglass drops to $45–$60 per sq ft but limits shape options. Vinyl liner is the budget choice at $35–$50 per sq ft.

4. Labor Rates in Fort Lauderdale

Pool construction labor in Broward County runs $65–$90 per hour, roughly 20% above the national average. Demand stays high year-round due to the climate. After a storm, rates can spike another 10% for emergency repairs to equipment.

5. Permits and Inspections

The City of Fort Lauderdale requires a building permit for any pool construction. Permit fees run $500–$1,500 depending on project valuation. The process includes a pre-dig inspection and final inspection. Your contractor should pull the permit.

6. Utilities and Access

Digging under existing utility lines requires hand digging or potholing. If you need to move gas lines, water meters, or electrical panels, expect $3,000–$8,000 in utility relocation fees. Narrow side yards can also increase equipment rental costs.

Material Cost per Sq Ft Lifespan

Gunite (Concrete) $65 – $85 50+ years
Pebble Finish $85 – $110 50+ years
Tile Coping $25 – $40 20–30 years
Saltwater System $2,500 – $4,500 10 years

Vinyl liner is the budget option. It comes in pre-formed shapes. The liner needs replacement every decade, which adds long-term cost. Best for tight budgets or rental properties.

Fiberglass offers a smooth finish and quick install. It comes in factory molds, so customization is limited. The shell is durable but can crack if the ground shifts.

Gunite (Concrete) is the standard for South Florida. You can build any shape or depth. It requires resurfacing every 10–15 years but is the most durable option.

Pebble finish is a premium upgrade to gunite. It uses natural stones mixed with cement for a textured look. It resists algae better than plaster and lasts longer. Expect to pay $85–$110 per sq ft.

Saltwater systems convert chlorine into a gentler form of salt. They cost $2,500–$4,500 to install initially but reduce chemical buying costs. The cell lasts 3–7 years before replacement.

Tile coping lines the pool edge. It prevents water from splashing onto the deck and adds a finished look. Ceramic or stone options range from $25–$40 per linear foot.

Florida-Specific Considerations

Building Codes: Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) governs all pool construction in Fort Lauderdale. Key requirements include secondary water barriers for equipment pads, specific electrical grounding, and GFCI protection for all outlets within 10 feet. These go beyond what most states require.

HVHZ Requirements: Fort Lauderdale is inside the High Velocity Hurricane Zone. Pool equipment enclosures and gazebos must meet wind load standards. Glass railings must be tempered or laminated. Your contractor should provide product approvals for all structural components.

Permits: Apply through the City of Fort Lauderdale Development Services portal. Turnaround is 10–15 business days for a standard permit. Budget $500–$1,500 for the fee. Inspections required for excavation, rebar, plumbing, electrical, and final.

Flood Zones: Many Fort Lauderdale homes are in AE or VE flood zones. Pool construction often requires elevation certificates for equipment pads. If the equipment pad is too low, you must build a raised deck. This adds $5,000–$10,000 to the cost.

Insurance Impact: A new pool increases your liability exposure. Most insurers require a $500,000 umbrella policy. Expect a premium increase of $500–$1,500 annually. Adding a safety fence or automatic cover can reduce this cost.

HOA: Many Fort Lauderdale communities restrict pool size, fencing, and equipment placement. Some require the pool to be screened or hidden from the street. Get written HOA approval before breaking ground — changing plans mid-job is extremely expensive.

Season: Best time to build is December through April — dry season, lower humidity, fewer rain delays. Avoid peak hurricane season (August–October). Rainy season (May–October) brings daily afternoon thunderstorms that delay excavation.

My Safe Florida Home: If your home was built before 2008 and is homesteaded, you may qualify for a free wind inspection. While pools don’t qualify for grants, safety covers and fencing upgrades may be part of a broader mitigation plan.

How to Save Money on Pool Construction in Fort Lauderdale

  1. Get at least 4 written quotes. Contractor pricing varies by 20–35% for identical scope in Fort Lauderdale. On a $100,000 job, that’s $20,000–$35,000 in potential savings. Make sure each quote itemizes excavation, shell, equipment, and permits separately.

  2. Schedule in dry season. December through April is slower for pool builders. You’ll have more leverage to negotiate and fewer weather delays. Some contractors offer 5–10% off-season discounts.

  3. Choose standard shapes over custom. A rectangle is faster to build than a free-form shape. Custom curves require more hand labor and rebar. The savings can be $15,000–$25,000 on a typical home.

  4. Bundle fencing with the pool. Installing safety fencing during construction is 40–60% cheaper than standalone projects. It ensures the pool meets code without needing separate contractors later.

  5. Check for utility rebates. Fort Lauderdale Power and Water sometimes offers rebates for high-efficiency pumps or solar heaters. Ask your contractor to check current programs before finalizing equipment.

When to Build Your Pool — Warning Signs

Your budget is under $60,000. This is not enough for a quality inground pool in Fort Lauderdale. Anything cheaper is likely a vinyl liner above ground or a very small concrete shell. Expect change orders to eat your budget.

Your insurance company is pressuring you. A pool increases liability. If your current policy won’t cover it, you may need to find a new carrier before starting. This can delay your project by weeks.

Your soil is unstable. If the site has clay or heavy organic matter, excavation is harder. You may need imported sand or a geotechnical report. This adds $5,000–$10,000 to the cost.

Your HOA is restrictive. Some communities ban pools or require specific fencing. Get the rules in writing before signing a contract. Fighting HOA restrictions mid-project is expensive.

How to Hire a Pool Contractor in Fort Lauderdale

  1. Verify their Florida license at MyFloridaLicense.com. You need a Certified Pool and Spa Contractor (CPC) or General Contractor (CGC) license.

  2. Confirm active insurance. Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $2 million) and workers comp. Call the insurer directly to verify.

  3. Check for a Fort Lauderdale local business tax receipt. This confirms they’re registered locally, not a storm chaser from out of state.

  4. Get 3–4 written estimates with line-item breakdowns — shell type, equipment brand, plumbing depth, electrical specs, warranty terms, and timeline.

  5. Check reviews on Google and BBB. Look for patterns in complaints, not just star ratings.

  6. Never pay more than 10% upfront. Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, excavation complete, shell complete, final inspection passed.

  7. Get everything in writing. Contract must include: scope, material specs, dates, payment schedule, warranty terms, permit responsibility, and cleanup plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an inground pool cost in Fort Lauderdale in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between $65,000 and $180,000. The average is around $115,000. Your actual cost depends on pool size, material choice, soil conditions, and whether structural repairs are needed.

Do I need a permit to build a pool in Fort Lauderdale?

Yes, always. Apply through the City of Fort Lauderdale Development Services portal. Budget $500–$1,500. Multiple inspections required. Never let a contractor skip the permit — it can void your insurance and create title issues when you sell.

How long does a pool installation take in Fort Lauderdale?

Standard concrete pools take 8–12 weeks from start to finish. Fiberglass pools take 4–6 weeks. Rainy season can add 2–4 weeks of weather delays.

Does a pool increase home value in Fort Lauderdale?

A pool recoups 40–60% of its cost at resale in South Florida. But the bigger value is lifestyle. Buyers in Fort Lauderdale often expect a pool. It makes your home easier to sell in a hot market.

Can I install my own pool in Florida?

Florida’s homeowner exemption allows it if you own and occupy the home. But you still need permits, inspections, and must meet all HVHZ code requirements. Insurance companies may refuse to cover a DIY pool. For most people, it’s not worth the risk.

Does homeowners insurance cover pool damage?

Storm damage (wind, hail, fallen trees) is typically covered minus your deductible — often 2% of insured value for hurricane claims in Florida. Normal wear and aging is never covered.

Bottom Line

Building an inground pool in Fort Lauderdale runs $65,000 to $180,000 for most homes, with gunite at the sweet spot of cost and performance. The most important thing to know: Fort Lauderdale’s high water table and permit requirements add cost but your new pool meets some of the toughest standards in the country — and it keeps you insurable in a market where carriers are dropping homes every day.
