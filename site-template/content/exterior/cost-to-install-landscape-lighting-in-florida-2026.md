# How Much Does It Cost to Install Landscape Lighting in Florida? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A professional landscape lighting installation in Florida costs between **$3,500 and $12,000** for most residential properties, with the average homeowner paying around **$6,800**. The biggest factors driving your price are square footage of the yard, fixture quality, and Florida Building Code electrical requirements.

Florida homeowners face unique challenges with outdoor lighting that other states do not. High humidity, salt air, and sandy soil require specific materials and installation methods to ensure safety and longevity.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$3,500 – $5,000** | 10–15 fixtures, basic path lights |
| Mid-Range | **$5,000 – $8,500** | 20–30 fixtures, LED upgrade, wiring |
| High-End | **$8,500 – $12,000+** | 40+ fixtures, automated controls, hardwired |

**Budget** packages usually include basic path lights and step lights. They use entry-level LED bulbs and standard low-voltage wire buried in shallow trenches. It meets code but lacks advanced controls.

**Mid-range** is the sweet spot for Florida homeowners. It includes uplights for trees, well lights for architecture, and smart controllers. Wiring is rated for wet locations and burial depth meets code.

**High-end** covers complex designs with automated timers, motion sensors, and dimmers. It uses commercial-grade fixtures rated for coastal corrosion. Includes electrical panel upgrades if needed.

These prices reflect Florida market rates as of early 2026 and include materials, labor, permits, and cleanup. They do not include major landscape reconstruction if soil conditions require it.

## What Affects the Cost in Florida

### 1. Yard Size and Layout

Lighting costs scale with the perimeter and square footage of your property. A small suburban lot under **5,000 sq ft** might need only 10 fixtures. Large lots over **10,000 sq ft** often require 30 or more lights to cover the perimeter and key features.

### 2. Fixture Type and Quality

Path lights are the cheapest option at **$100–$200 per light** installed. Uplights and well lights cost **$200–$400 per light**. High-end brass or bronze fixtures designed for salt air double that price.

### 3. Labor Rates in Florida

Licensed electricians in Florida charge **$75–$125 per hour**. This is higher than the national average due to licensing requirements and humidity-related work challenges. Specialized landscape lighting companies may charge more for design expertise.

### 4. Permits and Inspections

Florida requires an electrical permit for hardwired low-voltage systems connecting to the main panel. Permit fees run **$150–$400** depending on the county. Inspections verify burial depth and wire protection.

### 5. Trenching and Terrain

Digging through Florida's sandy soil is relatively easy. However, rocky areas or existing irrigation lines slow the process. Trenching adds **$2–$5 per linear foot** to the project cost if it requires heavy machinery.

### 6. Control Systems and Automation

Basic plug-in transformers cost little. Smart controllers that work with home automation apps add **$500–$1,500** to the system. Dimmers and photocells allow for energy savings but increase wiring complexity.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Fixture | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Path Lights | **$100 – $200** | 10–15 years |
| Spotlights | **$150 – $300** | 10–15 years |
| Well Lights | **$200 – $400** | 15–20 years |
| Deck Lights | **$100 – $250** | 10–15 years |
| Smart Control | **$500 – $1,500** | 5–10 years |
| Transformer | **$150 – $500** | 10–15 years |

**Path lights** line walkways and driveways. They are the most common fixture type in Florida. Expect to pay **$100–$200** per unit installed, including wire and transformer.

**Spotlights** highlight specific architectural features or trees. They use focused beams and require precise aiming. Installation takes longer than path lights, driving the **$150–$300** per unit price.

**Well lights** mount in the ground to wash light up onto walls or trees. They must be watertight to prevent corrosion. The **$200–$400** price includes the housing and bulb.

**Deck lights** install into railings or stairs for safety. They must meet specific voltage requirements for wet locations. The **$100–$250** range covers recessed housing and LED modules.

**Smart controls** allow remote scheduling via phone apps. They integrate with home security systems and smart speakers. The **$500–$1,500** cost includes the controller and wiring upgrades.

**Transformers** step down 120V power to 12V for the lights. High-quality units include timers and photocells. The **$150–$500** price depends on wattage capacity and weather rating.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) governs all electrical work. Low-voltage systems connecting to the grid require a licensed electrician. Wire must be rated for direct burial and protected in conduits where exposed.

**Corrosion Resistance:** Salt air along the coast accelerates metal corrosion. Fixtures must be rated for coastal use. Aluminum or stainless steel components are mandatory for homes in Miami-Dade or coastal Palm Beach.

**Permits:** Apply through your county's Building Department online portal. Turnaround is 5–10 business days. Budget **$150–$400** for the fee. Two inspections required: rough-in and final.

**Soil Conditions:** Florida's sandy soil drains well but shifts easily. Wire burial depth must be **12 inches minimum** to prevent erosion exposure. Rock layers may require jackhammering, adding cost.

**Humidity:** High humidity affects electrical connections. Junction boxes must be sealed with silicone. Transformers should be mounted off the ground to avoid ground moisture.

**Season:** Best time to install is December through April. Dry season avoids rain delays and muddy trenching. Avoid peak hurricane season (August–October) when contractors are busy with storm repairs.

**Utility Rebates:** Many Florida utility companies offer rebates for LED lighting upgrades. Check with FPL or Tampa Electric for current programs. These can offset **10–20%** of the material cost.

## How to Save Money on Landscape Lighting in Florida

1.  **Get at least 3 written quotes.** Contractor pricing varies by 15–25% for identical scope. On a **$6,000** job, that's **$900–$1,500** in potential savings. Compare material brands and warranty terms.

2.  **Choose LED over halogen.** LED bulbs use **75% less energy** and last longer. The upfront cost is higher, but you save **$100–$300 annually** on electricity bills over the system's life.

3.  **Bundle with other exterior work.** Combining lighting with deck installation or paver work saves on mobilization fees. Contractors often discount labor when multiple trades are scheduled.

4.  **Do your own trenching where safe.** You can dig shallow trenches for low-voltage wire if you stay under 12 inches deep. This saves **$1,000–$2,000** in labor but requires careful planning.

5.  **Check for utility rebates.** Contact your local power provider before starting. Rebates for LED upgrades or energy-efficient transformers can reduce material costs by **10–20%**.

## When to Install Your Landscape Lighting — Warning Signs

**Your yard feels unsafe after dark.** Poor lighting increases the risk of trips and falls. If you cannot see steps or obstacles clearly, it is time to upgrade.

**Your current fixtures are flickering.** Constant flickering indicates failing transformers or corroded connections. In Florida's humidity, corrosion happens faster than in other climates.

**Your energy bill is high.** Old halogen bulbs consume excessive power. Switching to LED reduces load on the system and cuts monthly costs significantly.

**You are selling your home.** Curb appeal drives value. Well-lit homes sell faster and often for higher prices. Lighting shows buyers the property layout clearly.

## How to Hire a Landscape Lighting Contractor in Florida

1.  **Verify their Florida license.** Search for a Certified Electrical Contractor (CQE) or Certified Mechanical Contractor (CMC). You need a license for electrical work in Florida.

2.  **Confirm active insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $1 million). Call the insurer directly to verify coverage.

3.  **Check for a local business tax receipt.** This confirms they are registered locally, not a storm chaser from out of state.

4.  **Get 3–4 written estimates** with line-item breakdowns — fixture brands, wire gauge, transformer capacity, permit responsibility, and warranty.

5.  **Check reviews on Google and BBB.** Look for patterns in complaints about weather damage or premature bulb failure.

6.  **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, wire install, final inspection passed.

7.  **Get everything in writing.** Contract must include: scope, material specs with weather ratings, dates, payment schedule, warranty terms, and cleanup plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does landscape lighting cost in Florida in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$3,500 and $12,000**. The average is around **$6,800**. Your actual cost depends on yard size, fixture quality, and permit requirements.

### Do I need a permit for landscape lighting in Florida?

Yes, usually. If the system connects to the main electrical panel, you need a permit. Budget **$150–$400**. Two inspections required. Never let a contractor skip the permit.

### How long does a landscape lighting installation take?

Average installations take **2–4 days**. Complex projects with automation can take **5–7 days**. Rainy season can add 1–2 days of weather delays.

### Does landscape lighting increase home value in Florida?

Lighting recoups **60–70%** of its cost at resale. But the bigger value is safety and curb appeal. A well-lit home looks more secure to buyers and neighbors.

### Can I install landscape lighting myself in Florida?

Homeowners can do low-voltage work if they own the home. But you still need permits for panel connections and must meet all electrical codes. It is risky for beginners.

### Does homeowners insurance cover landscape lighting?

Yes, usually under personal property coverage. But damage from hurricanes may have a separate deductible. Keep receipts and photos for insurance claims.
