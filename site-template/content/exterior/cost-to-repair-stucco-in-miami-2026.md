# How Much Does It Cost to Repair Stucco in Miami? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Stucco repair in Miami costs between **$1,500 and $15,000** for most projects, with the average homeowner paying around **$4,500**. The biggest factors driving your price are the extent of water damage, the type of stucco system, and Miami-Dade County's strict moisture control building codes.

Miami homes rely on stucco for a classic aesthetic and durability, but the tropical climate is tough on the material. Salt air, heavy rain, and humidity create conditions where cracks and moisture intrusion happen faster than in other regions.

This guide breaks down exactly what you will pay in 2026 for patching, re-texturing, or full wall repair. We cover the specific permit requirements for Miami-Dade and how to handle insurance claims for wind or water damage.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$1,500 – $3,000** | Small cracks, hairline repairs |
| Mid-Range | **$3,000 – $6,500** | Section repair, matching texture |
| High-End | **$6,500 – $15,000+** | Full wall re-texture, structural fixes |

**Budget** covers minor cosmetic fixes. This includes filling hairline cracks under **$100 per linear foot** and patching small areas under **50 square feet**. It does not address underlying water intrusion.

**Mid-range** is what most Miami homeowners need. It involves removing damaged sections, replacing lath or wire mesh, and applying new scratch and brown coats. Includes texture matching to existing walls.

**High-end** applies to large-scale failures or older homes needing full re-stucco. This often requires structural repairs to framing, moisture barrier replacement, and full exterior painting to ensure color uniformity.

These prices reflect Miami market rates as of early 2026 and include materials, labor, scaffolding, cleanup, and standard permits. They assume no major structural framing rot is found during the work.

## What Affects the Cost in Miami

### 1. Damage Size

Stucco is priced by the square foot for larger jobs or by the project for small patches. A small crack repair runs **$300–$800**. A section repair covering **100 to 200 square feet** runs **$3,000–$6,000**. If the damage covers more than **500 square feet**, the project moves into re-stucco territory costing **$8,000+**.

### 2. Stucco Type

Traditional 3-coat stucco is the standard in Miami for older homes. It costs **$8.00–$12.00 per sq ft** to repair. EIFS (Exterior Insulation and Finish Systems) is common on newer builds but harder to patch. EIFS repairs often require specialized materials at **$10.00–$15.00 per sq ft**.

### 3. Labor Rates in Miami

Specialized stucco finishers in Miami-Dade charge **$60–$90 per hour**. This is higher than the national average due to the specific skill required for texture matching. Labor accounts for roughly **60%** of the total project cost.

### 4. Permits and Inspections

Miami-Dade County requires a building permit for any stucco repair exceeding **$2,500** or involving structural framing. Permit fees run **$300–$1,000** depending on project valuation. The process requires an application via the county's online portal and a final inspection.

### 5. Scaffolding and Access

Many Miami homes are 2-story or have limited ground access. Renting scaffolding or lifts adds **$500–$1,500** to the project. If the work requires removing shutters or AC units, expect an additional **$200–$400** for re-installation.

### 6. Moisture and Mold Remediation

Water intrusion behind stucco is common in Florida. If you find mold or rot, remediation is mandatory before repair. Mold removal costs **$1,500–$3,000** depending on severity. Rotting framing replacement adds **$5.00–$8.00 per sq ft** to the stucco price.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Traditional 3-Coat | **$8.00 – $12.00** | 50+ years |
| EIFS (Synthetic) | **$10.00 – $15.00** | 30–40 years |
| Acrylic Finish | **$6.00 – $9.00** | 15–20 years |
| Cement Base Coat | **$4.00 – $7.00** | 40+ years |

**Traditional 3-coat** is the gold standard for Miami durability. It involves a scratch coat, brown coat, and finish coat. It is breathable and handles moisture well if installed correctly. Expect to pay **$8.00–$12.00 per sq ft** for repair.

**EIFS** systems use foam insulation boards underneath the finish. They look smooth and modern but are prone to water damage if not sealed perfectly. Repairs require specific adhesives and mesh at **$10.00–$15.00 per sq ft**.

**Acrylic Finish** is a pre-mixed synthetic coating applied over a base coat. It is flexible and resists cracking better than cement. It costs **$6.00–$9.00 per sq ft** and typically lasts 15–20 years before needing a refresh.

**Cement Base Coat** is the structural layer beneath the finish. It is rarely replaced alone unless the wall structure failed. If used for repairs, it costs **$4.00–$7.00 per sq ft**. Always ensure it is compatible with the finish coat.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code 8th Edition governs all exterior work in Miami. Chapter 25 covers exterior walls and requires moisture barriers behind stucco. Any repair must match the original assembly to maintain wind resistance ratings.

**HVHZ Requirements:** Miami-Dade is a High Velocity Hurricane Zone. Stucco systems must be tested for wind pressure and water intrusion. While stucco itself isn't always tested like windows, the attachment to the framing must meet HVHZ standards.

**Permits:** Miami-Dade County uses the ePlan portal for permit submissions. You need a licensed contractor to pull the permit for repairs over **$2,500**. Turnaround time is typically 5–7 business days. Budget **$300–$1,000** for fees.

**Insurance Impact:** Stucco cracks often hide wind or water damage. If your insurance adjuster finds water intrusion, they may deny the claim for normal wear. Document all cracks with photos. A new roof or window upgrade often pairs well with stucco repair for mitigation discounts.

**HOA:** Many Miami communities have strict architectural guidelines. Stucco color, texture, and finish must match existing homes. Get written approval from your HOA before starting work. Non-compliance can lead to fines or forced removal.

**Season:** Best time to repair stucco is November through April. Rain and humidity delay drying times for the base coats. Hurricane season (June–November) brings rain that can ruin wet cement. Schedule work before May if possible.

**My Safe Florida Home:** Homeowners built before 2008 may qualify for a free wind inspection. Grants up to **$10,000** are available for mitigation improvements. Stucco repair that addresses water intrusion often qualifies if it ties into broader hardening.

## How to Save Money on Stucco Repair in Miami

1. **Get at least 4 written quotes.** Contractor pricing varies by **20–30%** for identical scope in Miami. On a **$5,000** job, that's **$1,000–$1,500** in potential savings. Ensure each quote lists materials, labor, and permit fees separately.

2. **Schedule in dry season.** November through April is slower for contractors. You'll have more leverage to negotiate and fewer weather delays. Some contractors offer **5–10%** off-season discounts for early booking.

3. **Combine with painting.** If your stucco needs repair, it likely needs paint. Painting over repaired areas without matching the existing color looks terrible. Bundle the repair with a full exterior paint job at **$2.50–$4.00 per sq ft**.

4. **Check for insurance claim eligibility.** If the damage resulted from a recent storm, your insurance may cover part of the repair. File the claim before signing a contractor agreement. Be cautious of contractors who offer to "handle the insurance" — work with your adjuster directly.

5. **Do your own prep.** Clear the work area of furniture, plants, and grills. Remove window screens or shutters if possible. This reduces labor time and can save **$200–$500** on the final bill.

## When to Repair Your Stucco — Warning Signs

**Cracks wider than 1/8 inch.** Hairline cracks are normal expansion and contraction. Cracks wider than **1/8 inch** indicate structural movement or settling. These let water inside and should be fixed immediately.

**Soft spots when pressing the wall.** Tap the stucco with a hammer. A hollow sound or soft feel means the base coat has separated from the lath. This requires removal and replacement, not just patching.

**Water stains on interior walls.** Look behind your stucco repair zones. Brown water stains on drywall or peeling paint inside the house point to moisture intrusion through the exterior.

**Mold growth on the exterior.** Black or green spots on stucco indicate moisture retention. Miami's humidity feeds mold quickly. Mold can spread to framing and compromise structural integrity.

**Loose or missing texture.** If the finish coat is flaking off, the bond is broken. This exposes the base coat to UV and rain damage. Re-texturing is necessary to restore protection.

## How to Hire a Stucco Contractor in Miami

1. **Verify their Florida license** at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). You need a Certified General Contractor (CGC) or Certified Roofing Contractor (CCC) with stucco endorsement.

2. **Confirm active insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $1 million) and workers comp. Call the insurer directly to verify.

3. **Check for a Miami-Dade local business tax receipt.** This confirms they're registered locally, not a storm chaser from out of state.

4. **Get 3–4 written estimates** with line-item breakdowns — material brand, lath type, number of coats, texture, timeline, and warranty.

5. **Check reviews on Google and BBB.** Look for patterns in complaints, not just star ratings.

6. **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, base coat complete, finish coat complete, final inspection passed.

7. **Get everything in writing.** Contract must include: scope, material specs, dates, payment schedule, warranty terms, permit responsibility, and cleanup plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does stucco repair cost in Miami in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$1,500 and $15,000**. The average is around **$4,500**. Your actual cost depends on damage size, material type, and whether structural framing needs repair.

### Do I need a permit to repair stucco in Miami-Dade?

Yes, for repairs over **$2,500** or involving structural work. Apply through the county's ePlan portal. Budget **$300–$1,000**. Two inspections required: pre-cover and final. Never let a contractor skip the permit.

### How long does stucco repair take in Miami?

Small patches take **1–2 days**. Section repairs take **3–5 days**. Full wall re-stucco takes **10–14 days**. Drying time between coats requires **24 hours** per layer in humid weather.

### Does a new stucco repair increase home value in Miami?

A new stucco job recoups **60–70%** of its cost at resale. But the bigger value in Miami's market is protection — a home with intact stucco is less likely to have hidden water damage claims.

### Can I repair my own stucco in Florida?

You can do cosmetic patching yourself. But structural repairs and HVHZ compliance require a licensed contractor. Insurance companies may refuse to cover a homeowner-installed repair if it fails.

### Does homeowners insurance cover stucco repair?

Wind damage (hurricane) is typically covered minus your deductible. Normal wear and aging is never covered. If a storm caused the crack, file a claim. If it's settlement, pay out of pocket.
