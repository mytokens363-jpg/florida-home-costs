# How Much Does It Cost to Install Impact Windows in Boca Raton? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Installing impact windows in Boca Raton costs between **$18,000 and $45,000** for a typical single-family home, with the average homeowner paying around **$26,500**. Your final price depends heavily on the number of windows, the frame material, and the strict Miami-Dade NOA certification required for this coastal zone.

Boca Raton sits right on the edge of the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, meaning every single window must pass rigorous missile impact tests. This isn't just about keeping the rain out; it's about meeting the toughest building codes in the state to protect your home from Category 4 storms.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$18,000 – $24,000** | Vinyl frames, 10–12 standard windows |
| Mid-Range | **$24,000 – $32,000** | Aluminum frames, 15–20 mixed sizes |
| High-End | **$32,000 – $45,000+** | Custom aluminum, 25+ windows, large glass |

**Budget** projects usually involve standard vinyl frames with clear or lightly tinted glass. These cover about 10 to 12 average-sized windows on a modest home. It meets code but lacks premium finishes.

**Mid-range** is the most common choice for Boca Raton homeowners — aluminum frames with bronze or white finishes and low-E glass. This covers 15 to 20 windows, including sliding doors and larger picture windows.

**High-end** covers custom aluminum systems with bronze finishes, split-sash designs, and extensive glazing. This often includes 25 or more openings, custom shapes, and premium sound-dampening glass for coastal homes.

These prices reflect Boca Raton market rates as of early 2026. They include materials, professional labor, installation, disposal of old windows, and standard permit fees. They assume standard structural conditions without major framing repairs.

## What Affects the Cost in Boca Raton

### 1. Window Count and Size

The total square footage of glass is the primary driver. A typical Boca Raton home has 12 to 20 window openings. Small 24x36 inch windows cost **$600–$900** each installed. Large picture windows or sliding glass doors can range from **$1,200–$2,500** per unit. More openings mean more labor hours for removal, prep, and installation.

### 2. Frame Material Choice

Vinyl frames are the most affordable option at **$150–$250 per square foot** of opening. Aluminum frames, which are popular for their durability and slim profiles in Boca, cost **$200–$350 per square foot**. Wood-clad or fiberglass options are premium choices that can push costs to **$300–$450 per square foot**.

### 3. Glass Performance Features

Standard dual-pane glass is the baseline. Upgrading to low-E coating adds **$100–$200 per window** but reduces heat gain significantly. Adding a gas fill like Argon or Krypton costs another **$50–$100 per window**. Triple-pane glass is available but rare in impact applications due to weight and cost, adding **$300+ per unit**.

### 4. Labor Rates in Boca Raton

Skilled impact window installers in Palm Beach County charge **$60–$90 per hour**. This is higher than the national average due to the specialized skills required for HVHZ compliance. High demand during hurricane season can push labor rates up by **10–15%**.

### 5. Permits and Inspections

Palm Beach County requires a building permit for impact window replacements. Permit fees typically run **$300–$800** depending on the total project value and number of openings. The process involves submitting product data (NOA) and a structural review. Final inspection is mandatory before closing the permit.

### 6. Structural Modifications

Older Boca Raton homes often have wood frames that rot or shift. If the rough opening needs new framing, trim work, or sill repairs, expect to add **$500–$1,500 per opening**. This is a hidden cost that often surprises homeowners who assume a simple "drop-in" replacement.

### 7. Miami-Dade NOA Requirements

Boca Raton is adjacent to the High Velocity Hurricane Zone. While the city itself is not in the HVHZ, many insurance carriers and HOAs require Miami-Dade NOA certification for all impact products. This certification adds **$500–$1,500** to the material cost per window compared to standard non-certified products.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Vinyl | **$150 – $250** | 20–30 years |
| Aluminum | **$200 – $350** | 30–50 years |
| Wood-Clad | **$300 – $450** | 25–40 years |
| Fiberglass | **$280 – $400** | 35–50 years |
| Steel | **$350 – $500** | 40+ years |

**Vinyl** frames are the budget-friendly choice. They are energy-efficient and low maintenance but can look less premium than metal. They are rated for impact and last 20 to 30 years in the Florida sun.

**Aluminum** is the standard for South Florida. It offers a slim profile, high strength, and can be powder-coated in any color. It resists salt corrosion well and lasts 30 to 50 years. This is the most popular choice for Boca Raton homes.

**Wood-Clad** combines an aluminum exterior with a wood interior. It offers a classic look but requires more maintenance on the interior wood. It is the most expensive option and lasts 25 to 40 years.

**Fiberglass** is a high-performance material that expands and contracts less than vinyl or aluminum. It is incredibly strong and durable but carries a higher price tag. It can last 35 to 50 years with minimal upkeep.

**Steel** is the strongest option but rarely used for residential impact windows due to weight and cost. It is typically found in custom commercial or ultra-high-end residential projects. It can last 40+ years.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code 8th Edition mandates specific impact resistance ratings for all windows in coastal zones. In Boca Raton, windows must withstand 150 mph winds with missile impact testing. This ensures the glass won't shatter and create dangerous projectiles during a storm.

**Hurricane Zone:** While Boca Raton is technically in Zone 2 (140-150 mph), many insurance carriers treat it as Zone 3 (170+ mph) due to its proximity to the coast. Products must carry a Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval. Without this, your insurance may deny a claim for wind damage.

**Permits:** Apply through the Palm Beach County Building Department's online portal or the City of Boca Raton Building Division. The city has specific aesthetic review requirements for window colors and styles in historic districts. Budget **$300–$800** for permits and **10–14 days** for approval.

**Insurance Impact:** Installing impact windows can lower your windstorm deductible. Some carriers offer a **10–20% discount** on the wind portion of your premium. Others may require a new policy with a lower deductible. Always get a written quote from your insurer before starting the project.

**HOA Restrictions:** Boca Raton has many deed-restricted communities with strict rules on window appearance. You often cannot change the color of the frame or the style of the grid pattern. Get written HOA approval before ordering materials to avoid costly reorders.

**Season:** The best time to install windows is November through April. Avoid June through October due to hurricane season risks. Rain delays can stop work for days, and high humidity can affect adhesive curing times.

**My Safe Florida Home:** Homeowners with homesteaded properties built before 2008 may qualify for a free wind inspection. If your home passes, you can receive matching grants up to **$10,000** for impact window upgrades. This is a significant offset for the total project cost.

## How to Save Money on Impact Window Installation in Boca Raton

1. **Get at least 4 written quotes.** Contractor pricing for impact windows varies by **25–35%** for the same scope. On a **$25,000** job, that is **$6,000–$8,000** in potential savings. Ensure each quote lists the specific glass type, frame material, and NOA number.

2. **Bundle the entire house.** Replacing all windows at once is cheaper than doing them in stages. Contractors often offer **5–10%** off for full-home projects. It also minimizes disruption and allows for bulk material ordering.

3. **Choose aluminum over vinyl.** While vinyl is cheaper upfront, aluminum frames often last longer and require less maintenance in Boca's salty air. The long-term value of aluminum can outweigh the initial savings of vinyl.

4. **Check for rebates and grants.** The My Safe Florida Home program offers grants for impact window upgrades. Some utility companies also offer rebates for energy-efficient low-E glass. Check the state website before signing a contract.

5. **Schedule in the off-season.** November through March is the peak season for hurricane prep. Scheduling for late spring or early summer (before the rainy season peaks) might yield better pricing and faster scheduling.

## When to Replace Your Windows — Warning Signs

**Your windows are 20+ years old.** Most impact windows from the 2000s were not built to current HVHZ standards. If your home was built before 2005, your windows likely lack the necessary certification for modern insurance requirements.

**You see condensation between panes.** Foggy glass means the seal has failed. This reduces energy efficiency and allows moisture to rot the frame. In Boca Raton's humidity, this can lead to mold growth inside the wall cavity.

**The windows are hard to open or close.** If you have to force a window open, the frame has likely warped from heat and humidity. This compromises the weather seal and impact resistance. It also indicates the hardware is failing.

**Your insurance premiums are skyrocketing.** If your insurer is raising rates or threatening non-renewal, upgrading to impact windows is the fastest way to stabilize your costs. It proves your home is fortified against wind damage.

## How to Hire a Window Contractor in Boca Raton

1. **Verify their Florida license.** Check for a Certified Window Contractor (CCC) or General Contractor (CGC) license at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). Ensure the license is active and in good standing.

2. **Confirm Miami-Dade NOA certification.** Ask for the specific NOA numbers for the windows they plan to install. Verify these numbers on the Miami-Dade County website. No NOA means no insurance coverage.

3. **Check for local business tax receipt.** Boca Raton requires a local business tax receipt. This proves they are a legitimate local business, not a storm chaser.

4. **Get 3–4 written estimates.** Each quote should detail the window brand, glass type, frame color, labor, permits, and warranty. Look for line items that separate the glass cost from the frame cost.

5. **Check reviews and references.** Look for specific mentions of impact window installations. Ask for references from recent Boca Raton projects and visit them if possible.

6. **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida law limits deposits. Payments should be tied to milestones: deposit, delivery, installation, and final inspection.

7. **Get everything in writing.** The contract must include the scope, materials with NOA numbers, timeline, payment schedule, and warranty terms. Ensure it specifies who pulls the permits.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does it cost to install impact windows in Boca Raton in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$18,000 and $45,000**. The average is around **$26,500**. Your cost depends on the number of windows, frame material, and glass upgrades.

### Do I need a permit to install impact windows in Boca Raton?

Yes, always. Apply through the City of Boca Raton Building Division. Budget **$300–$800** for fees. Two inspections are required: rough-in and final. Never skip the permit to avoid insurance issues.

### How long does the installation take in Boca Raton?

A full home replacement typically takes **3–7 days**. Smaller jobs with 10–15 windows might take 2–3 days. Weather delays during the rainy season can extend this timeline.

### Do impact windows increase home value in Boca Raton?

Yes, they add significant value. A new impact window system can increase resale value by **10–15%** and make the home much easier to sell. The insurability factor is a major selling point for buyers.

### Can I install impact windows myself in Florida?

No, not legally. Florida requires a licensed contractor to pull permits for window replacements. Homeowners cannot install impact windows and still get the required NOA certification on the permit.

### Does insurance cover the cost of impact windows?

Generally, no. Insurance covers storm damage, not upgrades. However, installing impact windows can lower your annual premiums and protect your deductible from future claims.
