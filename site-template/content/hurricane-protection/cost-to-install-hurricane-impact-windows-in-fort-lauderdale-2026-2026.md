# How Much Does It Cost to Install Hurricane Impact Windows in Fort Lauderdale? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Installing hurricane impact windows in Fort Lauderdale costs between **$450 and $950 per window** for standard sizes, with the average homeowner spending around **$650 per unit**. The final price depends heavily on glass type, frame material, and the strict High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) requirements mandated by Broward County.

Fort Lauderdale sits right in the heart of the HVHZ, meaning every window you install must pass rigorous missile impact and cyclic pressure tests. You cannot simply buy "storm windows" off the shelf; they must be engineered to withstand 150 mph winds and flying debris.

This guide breaks down exactly what you will pay in 2026, how permits work in Broward County, and how these windows can lower your insurance premiums.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Per Window | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$450 – $600** | Vinyl frame, single-pane impact glass |
| Mid-Range | **$600 – $800** | Vinyl or aluminum, dual-pane, low-E |
| High-End | **$800 – $950+** | Fiberglass, dual-pane, custom sizes |

**Budget** windows use vinyl frames with a single layer of laminated safety glass. They meet code for wind pressure but offer less sound insulation and thermal efficiency compared to premium options.

**Mid-range** is the most common choice for Fort Lauderdale homeowners. It typically includes dual-pane glass with a low-emissivity (Low-E) coating to block heat, plus a thicker laminated interlayer for better noise reduction.

**High-end** covers fiberglass frames, which are stronger and more dimensionally stable than vinyl or aluminum. These often include custom shapes, larger sizes, and enhanced security features like multi-point locking systems.

These prices reflect installed costs in Fort Lauderdale as of early 2026. They include the window unit, professional installation, disposal of old windows, and standard permit fees. They do not account for structural repairs needed to the rough opening.

## What Affects the Cost in Fort Lauderdale

### 1. Window Size and Shape

Standard rectangular windows are the most affordable. Custom shapes like circles, arches, or large picture windows require specialized framing and glass cutting, pushing the price up significantly. A standard 36x36 inch window starts at **$450**, while a custom 60x80 inch picture window can exceed **$1,200**.

### 2. Frame Material

Vinyl frames are the most cost-effective at **$150–$250** per unit before glass. Aluminum frames cost slightly more at **$200–$300** but offer a sleeker, more modern look often preferred in coastal architecture. Fiberglass frames are the premium choice, ranging from **$300–$450** for the frame alone due to their superior strength and insulation properties.

### 3. Glass Technology

The glass is the most critical component. Single-pane laminated glass is the minimum requirement but offers poor energy efficiency. Dual-pane laminated glass with Low-E coating adds **$100–$200** per window but blocks 50% more heat and reduces noise. Argon gas fills between the panes add another **$25–$50** per window for better insulation.

### 4. Labor Rates in Fort Lauderdale

Skilled window installers in Broward County charge **$60–$90 per hour**. A professional crew can install 10 standard windows in a single day. Complex installations on higher floors or with difficult access require specialized equipment, adding **$200–$500** to the labor bill per window.

### 5. Permits and Inspections

Broward County requires a building permit for every window replacement project. The permit fee is typically **$50–$150** per window or a flat fee based on the total project value, often ranging from **$250 to $800** for a whole-house replacement. The process involves submitting product approval numbers and an on-site inspection after installation.

### 6. HVHZ Code Requirements

Fort Lauderdale is in the HVHZ, meaning every window must have a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA) or a Florida Product Approval. This certification process ensures the window can withstand 150 mph winds and 20 lb. missile impact. Products without this certification are illegal to install and will fail inspection, potentially costing **$500–$1,000** per window to replace.

**Bay and bow systems** involve multiple windows installed at angles to create a seating area. These are complex installations requiring structural support and custom flashing, often costing **$1,200 to $2,500** per unit.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) mandates that all windows in coastal zones must meet specific wind load requirements. In Fort Lauderdale, this means a minimum design pressure rating of 150 mph. The code also requires specific flashing details to prevent water intrusion during heavy rains.

**Hurricane Zone:** Fort Lauderdale is in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone. This is the strictest classification in Florida. Every product must have a Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval number. Installers must verify these numbers on the product before installation to pass inspection.

**Permits:** Broward County uses the ePlan portal for permit submissions. You must submit product data sheets, NOA numbers, and a site plan. The typical turnaround time is 7–14 business days. Inspection fees are included in the permit cost, but a final inspection is mandatory before the windows are considered "in service."

**Insurance Impact:** Installing impact windows can lower your wind insurance premium by **15–25%** in Florida. Many carriers offer specific discounts for homes with HVHZ-rated windows. Some insurers may even waive the 2% wind deductible if your home is fully protected with impact glazing.

**HOA:** Fort Lauderdale has many deed-restricted communities with strict aesthetic guidelines. HOAs often dictate frame colors, grid patterns, and window styles. Always get written HOA approval before ordering windows to avoid costly changes later.

**Season:** The best time to install windows is December through April. The dry season minimizes the risk of water damage during the installation process. Avoid peak hurricane season (August–October) when contractors are often booked with storm repairs.

**My Safe Florida Home:** Homeowners with older homes may qualify for free wind inspections and grants up to **$10,000** for impact window upgrades. This program is designed to help older homes meet modern wind safety standards and reduce insurance costs.

## How to Save Money on Impact Window Installation

1. **Bundle your order.** Contractors offer volume discounts for replacing all windows at once. Installing 15 windows instead of 5 can reduce the per-unit cost by **10–15%** due to economies of scale on labor and mobilization.

2. **Choose standard sizes.** Custom sizes require special manufacturing and longer lead times. Sticking to standard rough openings can save **$100–$200** per window. Measure your existing openings carefully to see what standard sizes fit.

3. **Ask about cash discounts.** Many local contractors offer **5–10%** off for cash payments to avoid credit card processing fees. This is a common negotiation tactic in the Florida home improvement market.

4. **Check for rebates.** Verify if your utility provider, like Florida Power & Light, offers rebates for energy-efficient windows. While federal tax credits have fluctuated, local utility incentives can sometimes cover **$50–$100** per window.

5. **Time it right.** Schedule your project for the off-season (May or November). Contractors are less busy than in winter and may offer better rates to keep their crews working.

6. **Keep the existing frames.** If your existing frames are structurally sound, ask if "insert" windows are an option. This involves installing the new glass and sash inside the old frame, which can save **$150–$300** per window on labor and disposal.

## When to Replace Your Windows — Warning Signs

**Your windows are 15+ years old.** Most impact windows have a lifespan of 20–25 years. Older windows may have failed seals or degraded framing materials, making them vulnerable during a storm.

**You see condensation between panes.** This indicates a broken seal in dual-pane windows, leading to poor insulation and potential glass failure. It also suggests the window is no longer energy efficient.

**Difficulty opening or closing.** If windows are stuck, warped, or hard to latch, they may not seal properly during high winds. This is a critical safety issue during hurricane season.

**Visible damage or cracks.** Even small cracks in the glass or frame can compromise the structural integrity of the window. Do not wait for a storm to find out a window is compromised.

**High energy bills.** If your AC is running constantly despite good insulation, your windows might be leaking conditioned air. Impact windows with Low-E coatings can significantly reduce cooling costs.

## How to Hire a Window Contractor in Fort Lauderdale

1. **Verify Florida licensing.** Ensure the contractor holds a Certified or Registered Window, Door, and Glazing (CDG) license or a General Contractor (CGC) license. Check status at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com).

2. **Check for HVHZ experience.** Ask specifically if they have installed windows in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone. They must be familiar with Miami-Dade NOA requirements and Broward County inspection processes.

3. **Request product NOA numbers.** A reputable contractor will provide the Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval number for every window they quote. If they hesitate, they may be using non-compliant products.

4. **Get multiple written quotes.** Prices vary by **20–30%** between contractors. Ensure each quote includes the window brand, glass specs, labor, permits, and warranty details.

5. **Verify insurance coverage.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability and workers comp. You do not want to be liable if a worker is injured on your property.

6. **Check references and reviews.** Look for recent projects in Fort Lauderdale or neighboring Broward counties. Ask about their punctuality and how they handled any issues during installation.

7. **Review the contract carefully.** It must specify the exact window model, frame material, glass type, installation timeline, and warranty terms. Never sign a contract with blank spaces.
