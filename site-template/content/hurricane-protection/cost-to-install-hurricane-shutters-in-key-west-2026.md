# How Much Does It Cost to Install Hurricane Shutters in Key West? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Installing hurricane shutters in Key West costs between **$8,000 and $25,000** for most single-family homes, with the average homeowner paying around **$14,500**. The biggest factors driving your price are shutter type, window count, and the City of Key West Historic District restrictions.

Key West sits in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone, which means your shutters must meet the strictest wind and impact standards in Florida. That adds cost — but it also means your home is protected against Category 4 and 5 storms.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Shutter Type | Cost Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Accordion | **$8,000 – $12,000** | Budget, full coverage |
| Roll-Down | **$12,000 – $18,000** | Convenience, daily use |
| Bahama | **$18,000 – $25,000+** | Aesthetics, ventilation |

**Accordion shutters** are the most affordable option. They mount on the exterior and slide open when not in use. They offer full coverage for the lowest price but can block light when deployed.

**Roll-down shutters** offer the best balance of convenience and cost. You deploy them with a crank or motor at the touch of a button. They integrate cleanly with Key West architecture but cost roughly **50%** more than accordion.

**Bahama shutters** are the premium aesthetic choice for Key West. They are hinged at the top and angle outward for ventilation. They are expensive but add property value and curb appeal in historic neighborhoods.

These prices reflect Key West market rates as of early 2026 and include materials, labor, mounting hardware, standard permits, and cleanup. They do not include structural reinforcement if your window frames need upgrade.

## What Affects the Cost in Key West

### 1. Window and Door Count

Shutter pricing is often based on the number of openings. A typical Key West home has 12 to 20 windows and 3 to 5 exterior doors. More openings mean more track, more hardware, and more labor. Each additional window door adds **$300–$800** to the total project.

### 2. Shutter Material and Grade

Materials must resist salt air corrosion. Marine-grade aluminum is standard for Key West. Cheaper steel or painted aluminum will rust within **3–5 years**. Marine-grade aluminum costs **$15–$25** more per linear foot but lasts the lifetime of the home.

### 3. Accessibility and Height

Many Key West homes have second-story windows that are hard to reach. Scaffolding or cherry pickers are required for second-story work. This adds **$500–$1,500** to the labor cost depending on roof pitch and accessibility.

### 4. Historic District Approval

Most Key West homes are in the Historic Preservation District. You must get approval from the Historic Preservation Board before installing exterior shutters. This adds **2–4 weeks** to the timeline and may restrict color or style choices.

### 5. Labor Rates in Key West

Labor is higher in Key West due to the island location. Contractors must pay for barge transport or ferry fees for equipment. Hourly rates run **$60–$90 per hour**, roughly **20%** above the mainland Florida average.

### 6. HVHZ Code Requirements

Key West is in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone. Every shutter must have a Miami-Dade NOA or Florida Product Approval. This ensures the product passed missile impact tests. Products without NOA numbers are illegal to install and will not pass inspection.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Window | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Accordion | **$400 – $800** | 20–30 years |
| Roll-Down | **$800 – $1,500** | 25–35 years |
| Bahama | **$1,200 – $2,500** | 30–50 years |
| Colonial | **$1,000 – $2,000** | 25–40 years |
| Plywood | **$100 – $300** | 5–10 years |
| Impact Glass | **$2,500 – $4,500** | 40+ years |

**Accordion shutters** are the budget king. They cover the largest area for the lowest price. They require storage space when folded back. Best for seasonal hurricane coverage.

**Roll-down shutters** offer the most convenience. Motorized versions connect to smart home systems. They are popular in modern Key West renovations. Expect to pay a premium for the motor mechanism.

**Bahama shutters** are the classic Key West look. They provide shade and ventilation during storms. They are heavy and require strong mounting brackets. Ideal for homeowners who want permanent curb appeal.

**Colonial shutters** are hinged on the sides and fold flat against the wall. They are less common in Key West but still a valid option. They offer a traditional aesthetic without the bulk of Bahama styles.

**Plywood** is the cheapest temporary option. You cut sheets to fit each window. It is labor-intensive to store and reinstall. Not code-compliant for permanent protection in most insurance policies.

**Impact glass** is a permanent alternative to shutters. It replaces the window entirely. It costs more upfront but removes the need to deploy anything. Best for new construction or full window replacement projects.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) governs shutter installation in Key West. Key requirements include minimum track thickness, anchor spacing, and wind load ratings. All hardware must be stainless steel or marine-grade to resist corrosion.

**Hurricane Zone:** Key West is in the HVHZ — the strictest wind zone in Florida. All shutter products must hold a Miami-Dade NOA. Products are tested with missile impact tests and cyclic pressure tests that simulate hurricane conditions. Your contractor should provide NOA numbers for every product.

**Permits:** Apply through the City of Key West Building Department at **700 Fleming Street**. Permit fees run **$300–$800** depending on project value. The process includes a review of historic district compliance. Turnaround is 10–20 business days.

**Insurance Impact:** This is the big one. Installed shutters can lower your windstorm premium. Many Florida insurers offer discounts of **10–25%** for approved hurricane protection. Check with your carrier before you buy — some only accept specific NOA numbers.

**HOA:** Key West has strict deed restrictions in many neighborhoods. Even if the city allows a shutter style, your HOA may not. Get written approval from your HOA board before signing a contract. Changing materials mid-job is extremely expensive.

**Season:** Best time to install shutters is **November through April**. Dry season ensures proper curing of sealants and adhesives. Avoid peak hurricane season (August–October). Rainy season (May–October) brings daily afternoon thunderstorms that delay projects.

**My Safe Florida Home:** If your home was built before 2008 and is homesteaded, you may qualify for a free wind inspection and matching grants up to **$10,000** toward hurricane hardening improvements including shutter upgrades.

## How to Save Money on Hurricane Shutter Installation in Key West

1. **Get at least 4 written quotes.** Contractor pricing varies by **20–35%** for identical scope in Key West. On a **$14,000** job, that's **$3,000–$5,000** in potential savings. Make sure each quote itemizes materials, labor, permits, and disposal separately.

2. **Schedule in dry season.** November through April is slower for shutter installers. You will have more leverage to negotiate and fewer weather delays. Some contractors offer **5–10%** off-season discounts.

3. **Choose accordion shutters if budget is tight.** The savings between accordion (**$400–$800/window**) and roll-down (**$800–$1,500/window**) can be **$4,000–$8,000** on a typical home.

4. **Bundle upgrades with the roof.** Adding hurricane straps or upgraded underlayment during a roof replacement is **40–60% cheaper** than standalone projects. The combined wind mitigation improvements can also cut your insurance premium by hundreds per year.

5. **Check for insurance claim eligibility.** If your existing shutters were damaged in a storm, your insurance may cover part or all of the replacement. File the claim before signing a contractor agreement. Be cautious of contractors who offer to "handle the insurance" — work with your adjuster directly.

## When to Install Shutters — Warning Signs

**Your roof is 15–20 years old.** Asphalt shingle roofs in South Florida degrade faster than in cooler climates due to intense UV, heat cycling, and salt air. A 20-year-old roof in Key West has taken more abuse than a 30-year-old roof in the Midwest.

**Your insurance company is pressuring you.** A non-renewal notice or letter requesting a roof inspection means the clock is ticking. Many Florida insurers are actively dropping homes with aging roofs.

**Visible corrosion on old hardware.** Check your existing tracks and brackets. Rust streaks on the siding or paint bubbling mean the metal is failing. Corrosion weakens the mounting strength and risks failure during a storm.

**Interior water stains or attic moisture.** Brown ceiling spots, bubbling paint, or damp insulation point to roof leaks. In Key West's humidity, even small leaks lead to mold fast. Multiple leaks usually mean the whole system has failed.

1. **Verify their Florida license** at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). You need a Certified Roofing Contractor (CCC), Registered Roofing Contractor (RC), or General Contractor (CGC) license.

2. **Confirm active insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $1 million) and workers comp. Call the insurer directly to verify.

3. **Check for a Key West local business tax receipt.** This confirms they are registered locally, not a storm chaser from out of state.

4. **Get 3–4 written estimates** with line-item breakdowns — material brand, underlayment type, number of squares, tear-off, permits, labor, timeline, and warranty.

5. **Check reviews on Google and BBB.** Look for patterns in complaints, not just star ratings.

6. **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, tear-off complete, mid-project, final inspection passed.

7. **Get everything in writing.** Contract must include: scope, material specs with NOA numbers, dates, payment schedule, warranty terms, permit responsibility, and cleanup plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does installing hurricane shutters cost in Key West in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$8,000 and $25,000**. The average is around **$14,500**. Your actual cost depends on window count, material choice, complexity, and whether structural repairs are needed.

### Do I need a permit to install shutters in Key West?

Yes, always. Apply through the City of Key West Building Department. Budget **$300–$800**. Inspections are required for permanent installations. Never let a contractor skip the permit — it can void your insurance and create title issues when you sell.

### How long does shutter installation take in Key West?

Accordion shutters take **1–2 days** for an average home. Roll-down or Bahama shutters take **3–5 days**. Historic district approval can add 2–4 weeks to the timeline before work begins.

### Does installing shutters increase home value in Key West?

Hurricane protection recoups **60–70%** of its cost at resale. But the bigger value in Key West's market is insurability — a home with approved shutters is dramatically easier to sell because buyers can actually get insurance on it.

### Can I install my own shutters in Florida?

Florida's homeowner exemption allows it if you own and occupy the home. But you still need permits, inspections, and must meet all HVHZ code requirements. Insurance companies may refuse to cover a homeowner-installed shutter system. For most people, it is not worth the risk.

### Does homeowners insurance cover shutter replacement?

Storm damage (wind, hail, fallen trees) is typically covered minus your deductible — often **2% of insured value** for hurricane claims in Florida. Normal wear and aging is never covered.
