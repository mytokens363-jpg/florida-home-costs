# How Much Does It Cost to Replace a Flat Roof in Davie? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A full flat roof replacement in Davie costs between **$15,000 and $35,000** for most single-story homes, with the average project landing around **$22,500**. The final price hinges heavily on the square footage of your roof, the specific membrane material chosen, and the strict Broward County permit requirements for low-slope systems.

Flat roofs in Davie are a distinct category from the standard pitched shingle roofs seen on neighboring streets. They require specialized drainage solutions, specific underlayment, and membranes that handle intense Florida UV exposure and sudden downpours.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|

**Budget** projects typically involve replacing an older modified bitumen system with a new 4-ply torch-down or cold-applied system. This is common on older mid-century homes in Davie where the existing structure is sound.

**Mid-range** covers single-ply TPO (Thermoplastic Polyolefin) or EPDM rubber on average-sized commercial-style roofs or large residential additions. It includes a complete tear-off, new insulation, and upgraded drainage scuppers.

**High-end** options include reinforced PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride) or standing seam metal flat roofs. These systems offer superior chemical resistance and longevity, often used on newer construction or high-value properties in Davie's gated communities.

These figures reflect Davie market rates for early 2026. They include materials, labor, waste disposal, and the necessary Broward County building permit fees. They assume standard structural conditions; rotting decking or major structural repairs will increase the total significantly.

## What Affects the Cost in Davie

### 1. Roof Size and Square Footage

Flat roofs are priced by the square (100 sq ft), but Davie homes often have complex overhangs and HVAC pads that reduce usable area. A typical single-story home in Davie has a flat roof section between 1,200 and 2,000 sq ft. At **$8.00–$14.00 per sq ft** installed, a 1,200 sq ft roof costs roughly **$10,000–$14,000** in materials alone, pushing the total project to **$15,000–$20,000**.

### 2. Material Choice

The material is the primary cost driver. Modified bitumen is the most affordable at **$6.50–$9.50 per sq ft** but requires torch application, which adds labor complexity. TPO is the market standard for **$7.50–$11.00 per sq ft**, offering excellent heat reflectivity. PVC is the premium choice at **$9.00–$13.00 per sq ft**, known for its durability against chemicals and oils.

### 3. Labor Rates in Davie

Roofing labor in Broward County is premium due to the specialized skill required for flat membranes. Skilled crews charge **$60–$85 per hour**. The work is physically demanding in the Florida heat, requiring early morning starts and strict safety protocols for torch work. Demand spikes after hurricane season, which can push labor costs up by **10–15%** during peak repair windows.

### 4. Permits and Inspections

Broward County requires a building permit for any flat roof replacement. The permit fee structure is based on the estimated value of the work, typically ranging from **$300 to $800** for residential projects. You must submit detailed plans showing drainage slopes and membrane specifications. The county requires a rough inspection before the membrane is installed and a final inspection upon completion.

### 5. Roof Complexity and Drainage

Flat roofs are never truly flat; they require a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot to prevent ponding water. If your roof has multiple HVAC units, skylights, or complex parapet walls, labor time increases. Re-routing drainage or installing new internal scuppers can add **$2,500–$6,000** to the project. Ponding water issues often require adding tapered insulation to create the necessary slope.

### 6. HVHZ and Wind Uplift

Davie is in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ). While the strictest wind codes apply to the coast, Broward County enforces enhanced wind uplift resistance for all roofs. Membranes must be fully adhered or mechanically fastened according to specific patterns. This "fully adhered" method uses more adhesive and labor than ballasted systems, adding **$1.50–$3.00 per sq ft** to the cost.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Modified Bitumen | **$6.50 – $9.50** | 15–20 years |
| TPO (Single-Ply) | **$7.50 – $11.00** | 20–25 years |
| EPDM Rubber | **$8.00 – $12.00** | 20–30 years |
| PVC (Single-Ply) | **$9.00 – $13.00** | 25–35 years |
| Metal (Standing Seam) | **$14.00 – $20.00** | 40+ years |
| Spray Polyurethane Foam | **$8.00 – $12.00** | 20–30 years |

**Modified bitumen** is the traditional workhorse for flat roofs. It comes in torch-down or cold-applied rolls. It's durable and repairable but requires open flames for installation, which can be a concern for some homeowners and insurance carriers.

**TPO** is the most popular choice in Florida due to its white, reflective surface. It reduces cooling costs by reflecting UV rays, a major benefit in Davie's intense summer heat. It is heat-welded at the seams for a watertight bond.

**EPDM** (black rubber) is highly durable but absorbs heat, which can increase cooling bills. It is often used on smaller residential flat roofs where energy efficiency is less of a priority than longevity. It is typically mechanically fastened or ballasted.

**PVC** is the premium single-ply option. It resists chemical degradation from HVAC units and is highly resistant to fire and wind uplift. It is ideal for complex roofs with many penetrations since it welds seamlessly around obstacles.

**Metal flat roofs** (standing seam) are becoming more common on modern Davie homes. They offer the longest lifespan and excellent water shedding, though they are the most expensive upfront investment.

**Spray Polyurethane Foam (SPF)** involves spraying a liquid foam that expands and hardens into a seamless layer, topped with a protective coating. It adds insulation and seals all cracks, making it excellent for retrofitting older roofs.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) mandates specific drainage slopes and waterproofing standards for low-slope roofs. All flat roofs in Davie must have a minimum slope of 1/4 inch per foot toward drains or scuppers. Ponding water that remains for more than 48 hours after rain is a code violation and a leading cause of premature failure.

**Hurricane Zone:** Davie falls under the Broward County wind zone requirements. Membranes must pass specific wind uplift tests. For fully adhered systems, the adhesive coverage must be at least 50% of the roof surface. Mechanical fasteners must be spaced tightly according to the wind zone maps.

**Permits:** Broward County's Building Division handles all permits. You can apply online via the Broward County ePlan portal or in person at the Building Safety Center in Lauderhill. The turnaround time for a standard flat roof permit is typically **7–14 business days**. Budget **$300–$800** for fees. Inspections are mandatory at the insulation stage and after membrane installation.

**Insurance Impact:** A new flat roof can significantly lower your windstorm insurance premium. Insurers often view a new, fully adhered membrane as a major risk reduction. However, some carriers are hesitant about flat roofs due to ponding water risks. Providing proof of proper drainage slope and a 20-year warranty can help secure better rates, potentially saving **$300–$600 annually**.

**HOA:** Many Davie neighborhoods have strict architectural review committees. Some HOAs prohibit torch-down applications due to fire risk, mandating cold-applied or TPO systems. Others may restrict roof colors, requiring light-colored, reflective materials. Always get written HOA approval before starting work to avoid costly rework.

**Season:** The best time to replace a flat roof is during the dry season, **November through April**. Summer thunderstorms are frequent and can delay membrane installation, leading to water damage if the roof is left open. High humidity in the summer can also affect the curing of adhesives and sealants.

**My Safe Florida Home:** Homeowners in Davie may qualify for the My Safe Florida Home program. If your roof is older than 10 years, a free inspection can identify vulnerabilities. You may receive a grant of up to **$10,000** for hurricane hardening, which includes upgrading drainage systems and securing the membrane to the deck.

## How to Save Money on Flat Roof Replacement in Davie

1. **Get 4+ detailed quotes.** Pricing for flat roofs varies wildly based on the installation method. A fully adhered TPO system might be quoted at **$10.00 per sq ft** by one contractor and **$13.00 per sq ft** by another. On a 1,500 sq ft roof, that's a **$4,500 difference**. Ensure every quote includes the same scope of work.

2. **Schedule during the off-season.** November through March is the peak roofing season in Florida, but late fall and early spring are slightly slower. Contractors may offer **5–10% discounts** to keep crews working during these shoulder months. Avoid August and September when storm damage backlogs are highest.

3. **Choose TPO over PVC if possible.** TPO offers 90% of the performance of PVC at a lower cost. For most residential applications in Davie, TPO is sufficient and saves roughly **$2,000–$4,000** on a standard project. Only choose PVC if you have specific chemical exposure concerns.

4. **Bundle drainage repairs.** If your roof has ponding water, fix the slope at the same time as the roof replacement. Adding tapered insulation during the tear-off is **50% cheaper** than trying to fix drainage later. This prevents future leaks and extends the roof's life.

5. **Check for tax credits.** While federal tax credits for residential roofs have fluctuated, energy-efficient roofing materials like reflective TPO or metal may qualify for local utility rebates. Check with Broward County or local utility providers for current incentive programs.

## When to Replace Your Roof — Warning Signs

**Your roof is 15+ years old.** Modified bitumen and older single-ply membranes degrade faster in Florida's UV environment. Cracking, blistering, and loss of granules are common signs. A 15-year-old flat roof in Davie is likely near the end of its functional life.

**You see standing water.** If water pools for more than 48 hours after a heavy rain, the drainage is compromised. This adds weight to the structure and accelerates membrane deterioration. Ponding water is a clear indicator that the roof system needs immediate attention.

**Visible cracks or blistering.** Walk the roof (safely) or look from a ladder. Cracks in the membrane or large blisters indicate that the layers are separating. These are entry points for water that will quickly damage the decking and insulation.

**High energy bills.** A failing flat roof loses its reflective properties and insulation value. If your AC bills spike in the summer without a change in usage, your roof may be failing to reflect heat or retain insulation.

## How to Hire a Roofing Contractor in Davie

1. **Verify Florida License.** Check the contractor's license at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). You need a Certified Roofing Contractor (CCC) or a General Contractor (CGC) with a roofing certification.

2. **Check for Bonding and Insurance.** Flat roof work involves heavy equipment and hazardous materials. Ensure the contractor has at least **$1 million** in general liability insurance and active workers' comp.

3. **Confirm Broward County Registration.** The contractor must have a valid local business tax receipt (formerly occupational license) issued by the City of Davie or Broward County.

4. **Request Specific Experience.** Ask to see photos of past flat roof projects in Davie. Flat roofs require different skills than shingle roofs. A shingle specialist may not understand tapered insulation or membrane welding.

5. **Get Line-Item Estimates.** The quote should separate material costs, labor, tear-off, disposal, and permit fees. Avoid lump-sum bids that hide costs.

6. **Avoid Large Upfront Deposits.** Florida law limits deposits to **10%** of the total cost. Never pay more than 10% before work begins. Tie payments to milestones like "tear-off complete" and "membrane installed."

7. **Review Warranty Terms.** Ensure the warranty covers both materials and labor. A 10-year or 20-year workmanship warranty is standard for quality flat roof installations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a flat roof replacement cost in Davie in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$15,000 and $35,000**. The average is around **$22,500**. The cost depends on the square footage, material choice (TPO vs. Modified Bitumen), and the complexity of the drainage system.

### Do I need a permit for a flat roof in Davie?

Yes, always. Broward County requires a permit for any flat roof replacement. The process takes **7–14 days** and costs **$300–$800**. Two inspections are required: one for the insulation/deck and one for the finished membrane.

### How long does a flat roof replacement take in Davie?

A standard residential flat roof takes **3–5 days** for a tear-off and re-installation. Complex roofs with multiple HVAC units or drainage issues may take **7–10 days**. Rain delays are common during the summer months.

### Does a new flat roof increase home value in Davie?

A new flat roof recoups **60–70%** of its cost at resale. However, the primary value is insurability. A new, code-compliant flat roof removes a major obstacle to getting homeowners insurance in Florida.

### Can I replace my own flat roof in Florida?

Homeowners can perform their own work if they own and occupy the home, but they must pull permits and pass inspections. Flat roof systems require specialized welding and adherence techniques that are difficult to master. Insurance companies often refuse to cover DIY flat roofs.

### Does homeowners insurance cover flat roof replacement?

Storm damage (wind, hail) is typically covered, but normal wear and tear is not. Florida windstorm deductibles are often **2% of the insured value**, which can be substantial. A new roof is an investment in long-term protection.
