# How Much Does It Cost to Resurface Pool in Naples? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A full pool resurfacing project in Naples costs between **$15,000 and $32,000** for most single-family homes, with the average homeowner paying around **$22,500**. The final price depends heavily on the finish type you choose, the total square footage of your pool, and the specific coastal soil conditions found in Collier County.

Naples sits in a unique climate zone where high humidity, intense UV rays, and salt air accelerate the wear on pool surfaces. Standard plaster often lasts only 7–10 years here compared to 10–15 years in drier states. Choosing the right material now prevents the need for a premature redo next season.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$15,000 – $19,000** | Standard white plaster, 18x36 pool |
| Mid-Range | **$19,000 – $26,000** | Quartz aggregate, 20x40 pool |
| High-End | **$26,000 – $32,000+** | Pebble Tec or glass, complex shape |

**Budget** options use standard white plaster (white marble dust and cement). It is the most affordable option but shows staining and wear quickly in Naples' hard water. It fits smaller pools under 250 square feet of surface area.

**Mid-range** quartz finishes mix crushed quartz with the cement base. This creates a harder, more durable surface that resists staining and lasts 12–15 years. It is the most popular choice for Naples homeowners balancing cost and longevity.

**High-end** pebble or glass finishes use exposed natural stones or glass beads. These are the most durable options, lasting 20+ years, and provide a premium aesthetic. They command a higher price due to material costs and the extra labor required for installation.

These prices reflect Naples market rates for early 2026 and include water drainage, surface preparation, new finish application, tile replacement (if included), and water refill. They do not cover major structural cracks that require gunite repair before resurfacing.

## What Affects the Cost in Naples

### 1. Pool Size and Shape

Pool resurfacing is priced by the square foot of the interior surface, not just the length and width. A standard 15x30 lap pool has a smaller surface area than a 15x30 pool with a deep end and a tanning ledge. Larger surface areas require more material and labor, pushing costs toward the higher end of the range.

Complex shapes with curves, tanning ledges, and built-in spas increase labor time significantly. A simple rectangle is the most efficient to resurface. Adding a spa or a swim-up bar can add **$2,000–$5,000** to the total project cost due to the extra detailing required.

### 2. Material Choice

The finish material is the primary driver of your final bill. Standard plaster is the cheapest at **$5.00–$7.00 per sq ft**. Quartz aggregate jumps to **$8.00–$12.00 per sq ft**. Pebble and glass finishes are premium, ranging from **$12.00–$18.00 per sq ft**.

Naples homeowners often prefer quartz or pebble because standard plaster degrades faster in the local environment. The harder materials resist the chemical imbalance often caused by hard water and high evaporation rates common in Southwest Florida.

### 3. Labor Rates in Collier County

Pool labor in Naples is among the highest in Florida due to the concentration of high-end homes and the specialized skills required. Experienced pool crews charge **$65–$90 per hour** for skilled finishers. General laborers cost less, but the finish application is a highly specialized trade.

Demand for pool work peaks in the spring before the summer swim season. Booking in late fall or winter often secures better pricing and faster scheduling. During peak season, contractors may add a **10–15% rush fee** to cover overtime and material delivery costs.

### 4. Permits and Inspections

Collier County requires a building permit for any pool resurfacing project that involves structural changes or complete drain-downs. Permit fees typically range from **$300 to $800** depending on the pool's volume and the contractor's classification.

The process involves submitting a site plan and engineering details if the waterline tile or coping is being replaced. Inspections are required at the pre-plaster stage and upon final completion. Skipping the permit can lead to fines and issues when selling your home.

### 5. Drainage and Disposal

Draining a pool in Naples requires careful planning. You cannot simply dump the water into the street or storm drain due to local ordinances regarding water quality and salinity. You must pump the water to a licensed disposal site or a sanitary sewer system with a permit.

Disposal fees in Collier County run **$500–$1,500** depending on the volume of water and the distance to the disposal facility. Some contractors include this in their quote, while others charge it as a separate line item. Always clarify who handles the water removal logistics.

### 6. Coastal and Soil Conditions

Naples has unique soil conditions that can affect pool stability. The area features high water tables and sandy soil that shifts. If your pool shell has shifted or cracked due to soil movement, resurfacing alone is not enough.

You may need to inject grout or repair the gunite shell before the new finish goes on. Structural repairs can add **$3,000–$10,000** to the project. A professional inspection is critical to determine if the pool structure is sound before committing to a resurface.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Standard Plaster | **$5.00 – $7.00** | 7–10 years |
| Quartz Aggregate | **$8.00 – $12.00** | 12–15 years |
| Pebble Tec | **$12.00 – $16.00** | 20+ years |
| Glass Finish | **$15.00 – $20.00** | 25+ years |
| Exposed Aggregate | **$10.00 – $14.00** | 15–20 years |
| Polished Plaster | **$7.00 – $9.00** | 8–12 years |

**Standard plaster** is the entry-level option. It provides a smooth, classic white look but is porous and prone to staining from minerals in Naples' hard water. It requires regular acid washing and brushing to maintain its appearance.

**Quartz aggregate** embeds crushed quartz stones into the plaster mix. This creates a surface that is harder than standard plaster and more resistant to chemicals and stains. It offers a subtle sparkle and is the most common upgrade in the Naples market.

**Pebble Tec** and similar exposed aggregate finishes use natural river pebbles. These are the most durable options available, resisting wear from foot traffic and cleaning brushes. The texture is rougher, which some swimmers find less comfortable than quartz, but the longevity is unmatched.

**Glass finishes** use crushed glass particles for a luxurious, shimmering effect. They are extremely smooth and resistant to algae growth due to their non-porous nature. However, they are the most expensive option and require a highly skilled installer to ensure a uniform finish.

**Exposed aggregate** uses a blend of larger stones for a rustic, natural look. It is a middle ground between quartz and pebble, offering good durability at a moderate price point. It is popular in more traditional Naples estate styles.

**Polished plaster** is a refined version of standard plaster with a smoother texture. It is slightly more durable than basic plaster but still requires more maintenance than quartz or pebble options. It is best for pools that are used primarily for lounging rather than active swimming.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code requires pool resurfacing to meet specific safety standards regarding slip resistance and structural integrity. In Collier County, any work involving the pool shell must comply with the 8th Edition code, including requirements for new waterline tiles and coping if they are removed.

**HVHZ:** While Naples is not in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) like Miami-Dade, it is in a coastal zone with high wind exposure. Pool equipment and covers must be secured to withstand Category 3 hurricanes. Resurfacing does not directly affect wind codes, but the structural shell must be sound.

**Permits:** Apply for permits through the Collier County Building Department online portal or in person at the Marco Island or Naples offices. The turnaround time is typically **5–10 business days** for standard residential projects. Budget **$300–$800** for the permit fee, which includes plan review and inspections.

**Insurance Impact:** A resurfaced pool does not typically lower your homeowners insurance premium directly, but a well-maintained pool reduces liability risks. If the resurfacing includes updating old plumbing or electrical components, you may qualify for minor discounts. However, the primary value is in preventing costly repairs that could lead to claims.

**HOA:** Naples has some of the strictest Homeowners Association rules in the state. Many communities have specific color palettes for pool decks, tile, and coping materials. Some HOAs prohibit certain pebble finishes or require approval for any exterior changes. Always get written HOA approval before starting work to avoid fines.

**Season:** The best time to resurface a pool in Naples is late fall or early winter, from **November to February**. The cooler weather allows the plaster to cure properly without cracking from the intense heat. Avoid scheduling during hurricane season (June to November) due to the risk of rain delays.

**My Safe Florida Home:** This program focuses on wind mitigation for the main structure. While it does not directly fund pool resurfacing, if your pool pump house or equipment pad is being upgraded as part of a larger hardening project, you might qualify for a small grant. Check the program's current eligibility criteria.

## How to Save Money on Pool Resurfacing in Naples

1. **Get at least 4 written quotes.** Pool contractor pricing varies by **25–40%** for identical scopes in Naples. On a **$20,000** job, this difference is **$5,000–$8,000**. Ensure each quote details the specific brand of material, labor hours, and disposal fees.

2. **Bundle with deck or equipment work.** If you need to replace pool coping or update the pump equipment, do it all at once. Contractors often offer a **10–15% discount** on labor when multiple services are performed in a single project window.

3. **Choose quartz over pebble.** Quartz aggregate provides a significant upgrade over standard plaster for a fraction of the cost of pebble finishes. The savings between quartz (**$8–$12/sq ft**) and pebble (**$12–$16/sq ft**) can be **$3,000–$6,000** on a typical pool.

4. **Schedule in the off-season.** Pool contractors are busiest in the spring. Booking in December or January often secures a lower price and faster start date. You avoid the **10–15% rush fees** that apply during peak swim season.

5. **Do the prep work yourself.** If you are handy, you can clear the deck area, remove old furniture, and move lightweight equipment. This can save on labor costs, but do not attempt to drain the pool yourself unless you have a licensed contractor handle the water disposal.

## When to Resurface Your Pool — Warning Signs

**Your pool surface is flaking or rough.** If you can feel the plaster crumbling under your feet or see chunks of white material in the filter, the surface is failing. This is a common sign that the plaster has reached the end of its 7–10 year lifespan in Naples.

**Stains won't scrub off.** Persistent green or black stains that resist acid washing indicate the plaster is porous and stained deeply. This often happens when the plaster has lost its integrity and minerals from the water have penetrated the surface.

**Visible cracks or delamination.** Hairline cracks are normal, but large cracks or areas where the finish is separating from the gunite shell are serious. Water leaking behind the shell can cause structural damage to your yard and foundation.

**Your water chemistry is constantly unbalanced.** If you find yourself adding chemicals constantly just to keep the water clear, the rough surface of an old pool may be harboring bacteria. A smooth new surface makes balancing water easier and cheaper.

## How to Hire a Pool Contractor in Naples

1. **Verify their license.** Check the Florida DBPR website for a valid **CSE (Swimming Pool, Spa, and Hot Tub Contractor)** license. General contractors cannot legally perform pool resurfacing without this specific classification.

2. **Confirm insurance coverage.** Ask for a certificate of insurance showing general liability of at least **$1 million** and workers comp. Call the insurer to verify the policy is active.

3. **Check local references.** Ask the contractor for 3 recent references from Naples or Marco Island. Drive by those homes if possible to see how the finish is holding up after a year.

4. **Get detailed written estimates.** The quote must specify the exact material brand, thickness of the plaster, number of coats, and warranty terms. Vague quotes like "resurface pool" are red flags.

5. **Review the warranty.** A reputable contractor should offer a **5–10 year warranty** on labor and a manufacturer warranty on materials. Understand what voids the warranty, such as improper water chemistry maintenance.

6. **Avoid cash-only deals.** Never pay the full amount in cash. Payment should be tied to milestones: deposit, start of work, completion of drain-down, and final inspection.

7. **Check for HOA compliance.** Ask the contractor if they are familiar with your specific HOA rules. A good contractor will know the restrictions and help you navigate the approval process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a pool resurface cost in Naples in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$15,000 and $32,000**. The average cost is around **$22,500**. Your specific price depends on pool size, material choice, and whether structural repairs are needed.

### Do I need a permit to resurface my pool in Collier County?

Yes, a permit is required for any resurfacing project. Apply through the Collier County Building Department. Budget **$300–$800** for fees. Inspections are required at the pre-plaster stage and final completion.

### How long does a pool resurfacing project take in Naples?

The actual resurfacing process takes **3–5 days** for a standard pool. This includes draining, cleaning, prep work, application, and curing time. Water refill and chemical balancing add another **1–2 days**.

### Does a new pool surface increase home value?

A resurfaced pool recoups **50–60%** of its cost at resale. The bigger value in Naples is curb appeal and the elimination of immediate maintenance costs. It signals to buyers that the pool is ready to use immediately.

### Can I resurface my pool myself?

No, you cannot legally resurface a pool yourself in Florida. This work requires a licensed CSE contractor. DIY attempts often fail due to improper mixing and curing, leading to costly repairs and potential safety hazards.

### Does homeowners insurance cover pool resurfacing?

No, insurance does not cover routine maintenance or wear and tear. Resurfacing is considered a home improvement expense. Insurance only covers resurfacing if the damage was caused by a covered peril like a falling tree or lightning strike.
