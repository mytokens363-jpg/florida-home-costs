# How Much Does It Cost to Resurface Pool in Boca Raton 2026? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A full pool resurfacing project in Boca Raton costs between **$12,500 and $28,000** for an average-sized backyard pool. The typical homeowner pays around **$18,500**. Your final price depends heavily on the finish material you choose, the condition of the underlying gunite, and Palm Beach County's strict permit requirements.

Boca Raton sits right on the Atlantic coast, meaning your pool faces constant exposure to salt air and high humidity. This environment accelerates the wear on standard white plaster, often shortening its lifespan compared to inland Florida homes.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$12,500 – $15,000** | Standard white plaster, basic prep |
| Mid-Range | **$15,000 – $20,000** | Quartz aggregate, upgraded equipment |
| High-End | **$20,000 – $28,000+** | PebbleTec, extensive repairs, tile work |

**Budget** covers standard white plaster (marcite) on a pool in decent structural condition. It includes draining, cleaning, minor crack repair, and the new finish. It is the most affordable option but requires more maintenance over time.

**Mid-range** typically involves a quartz aggregate finish. This adds sparkle and durability to the pool surface. It also often includes upgrading the main drain cover to a safety-compliant model and replacing old ladders or steps.

**High-end** projects use premium pebble finishes like PebbleTec or PebbleSheen. These surfaces last 20+ years and resist staining. This tier often includes repairing structural cracks in the gunite, replacing the entire coping, and installing new tile lines.

These prices reflect Boca Raton market rates for early 2026 and include labor, materials, equipment rental, disposal of old plaster, and Palm Beach County permit fees. They assume the pool shell is structurally sound enough to hold a new finish.

## What Affects the Cost in Boca Raton

### 1. Pool Size and Depth

Pool resurfacing is priced by the square footage of the interior surface area, not just the water volume. A typical Boca Raton residential pool ranges from 400 to 600 square feet. Larger pools with deep ends or custom shapes require more material and labor. A 400 sq ft pool might cost **$14,000**, while a 700 sq ft custom lagoon can exceed **$25,000**.

### 2. Finish Material Choice

The surface material is the biggest price driver. Standard white plaster is the cheapest at **$25–$35 per sq ft**. Quartz aggregate adds a layer of crushed granite for durability, running **$35–$50 per sq ft**. Premium pebble finishes are the most expensive, costing **$50–$75 per sq ft**. The choice affects not just the upfront cost but the long-term maintenance budget.

### 3. Structural Repairs Needed

Many older Boca Raton pools have hairline cracks or delamination in the original gunite. If the shell is compromised, you cannot just apply a new finish. You must repair the structural cracks, which can add **$2,000 to $8,000** depending on the severity. A professional inspection during the quote phase is critical to uncover hidden structural issues.

### 4. Permit and Inspection Fees

Palm Beach County requires a building permit for any pool resurfacing project. The fee is calculated based on the total project value, typically ranging from **$150 to $450**. The process involves a plan review and a final inspection to ensure safety standards are met. Skipping this step can lead to fines and issues when selling the home.

### 5. Equipment and Access

If the pool pump, filter, or heater needs replacement during the resurfacing, costs rise significantly. A new high-efficiency pump can add **$1,500–$2,500**. Access is another factor; pools behind narrow gates or with limited crane access may require manual labor to remove old debris, adding **$500–$1,000** to the labor bill.

### 6. Coastal Zone Requirements

Boca Raton homes are often subject to strict environmental regulations due to the proximity to the Intracoastal Waterway and the ocean. While resurfacing itself is less regulated than new construction, the disposal of old plaster and water must follow county environmental guidelines. Some HOAs also restrict the color palette of new finishes to match community aesthetics.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| White Plaster | **$25 – $35** | 7–10 years |
| Quartz Aggregate | **$35 – $50** | 10–15 years |
| Pebble Finish | **$50 – $75** | 15–25 years |
| Polished Plaster | **$40 – $60** | 10–15 years |
| Fiberglass Overlay | **$45 – $65** | 10–15 years |

**White plaster** is the traditional choice. It feels smooth but is porous, making it prone to algae growth and staining. In Boca Raton's humid climate, it often turns green or develops rough spots within 7 years. It is the go-to for budget-conscious homeowners.

**Quartz aggregate** mixes crushed quartz stones with white plaster. This creates a harder, more durable surface that resists etching from acidic water. It offers a subtle sparkle and is a popular mid-range upgrade for Boca Raton families.

**Pebble finishes** use natural river pebbles or glass beads mixed into the plaster. They are extremely durable and textured, which some swimmers love and others find rough on bare feet. They are the best choice for longevity in a coastal environment.

**Polished plaster** offers a smooth finish similar to standard plaster but with added polymers for better stain resistance. It sits between white plaster and quartz in price, offering a middle ground for those who want a smooth feel without the rapid degradation.

**Fiberglass overlay** is a newer technology where a fiberglass mat is embedded in the plaster. It creates a seamless, non-porous surface that resists algae better than any other option. It is gaining popularity in Boca Raton for its low maintenance requirements.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code 8th Edition (2023) mandates specific safety standards for all pool work. This includes the requirement for anti-entrapment drain covers that meet the Virginia Graeme Baker Pool and Spa Safety Act. Any resurfacing job must ensure the main drain is compliant before the permit is issued.

**HVHZ Impact:** While Boca Raton is not in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) like Miami-Dade, it is in a coastal high-wind zone. This affects the design of the pool equipment pad and any overhead structures. All new equipment must be securely anchored to withstand potential wind events.

**Permits:** Palm Beach County handles permits through the online portal "Palm Beach County ePlan Services." The turnaround time for a standard pool resurfacing permit is typically 7–14 business days. You must submit a scope of work and proof of insurance before work begins.

**Insurance Impact:** A new pool surface can improve your homeowner's insurance eligibility. Some carriers view a new pool as a reduced liability risk, potentially lowering premiums by **5–10%**. However, if the resurfacing involves significant structural repairs, the insurance company may require a new inspection of the entire pool structure.

**HOA Restrictions:** Boca Raton has some of the most restrictive Homeowners Associations in the state. Many communities have deed restrictions that dictate the exact color of the plaster or require specific tile colors. Always get written approval from your HOA architectural review committee before ordering materials.

**Season:** The best time to resurface a pool in Boca Raton is late fall or early spring. Avoid the peak summer months (June–September) when afternoon thunderstorms are daily. Rain can delay the curing process for new plaster, leading to potential defects or delays in reopening.

**My Safe Florida Home:** If your pool is part of a larger home hardening project, you might qualify for the My Safe Florida Home program. While primarily focused on the house structure, some rebates cover safety upgrades like pool fencing or drain covers that are part of the resurfacing scope.

## How to Save Money on Pool Resurfacing in Boca Raton

1. **Bundle the work with equipment upgrades.** Replacing your pump and filter while the pool is drained saves on labor mobilization fees. Contractors often offer a **10–15% discount** if you do the resurfacing and equipment replacement in one project.

2. **Choose quartz over pebble if budget is tight.** The price difference between quartz (**$35–$50/sq ft**) and pebble (**$50–$75/sq ft**) is significant. Quartz provides 80% of the durability for 60% of the cost, making it a smart financial choice for many homeowners.

3. **Schedule during the off-season.** March and April are often slower for pool contractors before the summer rush. Scheduling your project during this window can give you leverage to negotiate a **5–10% discount** on labor costs.

4. **Get multiple detailed quotes.** Pricing varies wildly among Boca Raton pool companies. One quote might be **$16,000** while another is **$22,000** for the same scope. Ensure every quote includes the same line items: drain cover, plaster type, and permit fees.

5. **Check for rebates on energy-efficient pumps.** Replacing an old pump with a new variable-speed model might qualify for a utility rebate from Florida Power & Light. These rebates can offset **$300–$500** of your total project cost.

## When to Resurface Your Pool — Warning Signs

**Your plaster is pitted or rough.** Run your hand along the pool wall. If it feels like sandpaper or you can see small holes, the plaster is degrading. This texture encourages algae to grow and makes the water difficult to balance.

**The color is uneven or stained.** If the pool has gray spots, green patches, or rust stains that cleaning cannot remove, the plaster is likely porous and failing. Boca Raton's hard water can also cause scale buildup that ruins the finish.

**You are losing water through the walls.** If you have ruled out evaporation and leaks in the plumbing, the water might be seeping through cracks in the old plaster. This is a sign the structural shell is compromised.

**The pool is 10+ years old.** Standard white plaster in South Florida typically lasts 7 to 10 years. If your pool is older and showing signs of wear, waiting longer will only increase the cost of repairs.

## How to Hire a Pool Contractor in Boca Raton

1. **Verify their Florida license.** Check the license at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). You need a Certified Pool and Spa Contractor (CPC) or a General Contractor (CGC) with pool specialty.

2. **Check for local bonding.** Ensure the contractor is bonded in Palm Beach County. This protects you if they fail to complete the job or pay their subcontractors.

3. **Ask for recent Boca Raton references.** Request a list of three recent clients in your specific neighborhood. Call them to ask about the contractor's punctuality, cleanup habits, and how the pool held up after six months.

4. **Get a detailed written contract.** The contract must specify the exact plaster brand, the thickness of the application, the type of drain cover, and the warranty terms. Verbal agreements are not enforceable in Florida.

5. **Confirm permit responsibility.** The contract should state that the contractor will pull all necessary permits and schedule inspections. Do not let them work without a permit; it can void your insurance.

6. **Review the warranty.** A reputable contractor should offer a minimum 5-year warranty on the plaster work. Read the fine print to see what voids the warranty, such as improper water chemistry.

7. **Avoid cash-only deals.** Never pay the full amount in cash. Use a check or credit card that allows you to dispute charges if the work is not completed to standard.

Yes, you can live in your home. The work is contained to the backyard. However, the pool will be unusable for **10 to 14 days** while the new plaster cures. You will need to plan for alternative swimming arrangements.

A resurfaced pool adds value by improving curb appeal and reducing maintenance costs for potential buyers. It can increase the resale value by **$5,000 to $10,000**, especially in a competitive market like Boca Raton.

Standard white plaster lasts **7–10 years**. Quartz aggregate lasts **10–15 years**. Premium pebble finishes last **15–25 years**. The choice depends on your budget and how long you plan to stay in the home.
