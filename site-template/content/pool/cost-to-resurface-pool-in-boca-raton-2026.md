# How Much Does It Cost to Resurface a Pool in Boca Raton? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A professional pool resurfacing project in Boca Raton costs between **$6,000 and $14,000** for most standard-sized pools. The average homeowner pays around **$9,500** for a full replastering or pebble finish.

The price depends heavily on your pool's square footage, the material you select, and whether you need to repair plumbing or coping during the job. Palm Beach County requires specific permits and inspections that add to the timeline and cost.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$6,000 – $8,000** | Standard white plaster, small pool |
| Mid-Range | **$8,000 – $11,000** | Quartz aggregate, 15x30 avg pool |
| High-End | **$11,000 – $14,000+** | Pebble Tec, tile coping, repairs |

**Budget** projects cover a standard white plaster resurfacing on a smaller pool under 400 sq ft. This includes draining, acid washing, and basic plaster application. It is the most affordable option but requires maintenance every 5–7 years.

**Mid-range** typically involves quartz aggregate finishes. These mixes stone dust into the plaster for better durability and texture. This is the most common choice for Boca Raton homeowners seeking a balance of cost and longevity.

**High-end** covers premium pebble finishes or full tile installations. It also includes necessary structural repairs like fixing cracks in the shell or replacing old coping stones before resurfacing.

These prices reflect Boca Raton market rates as of early 2026. They include labor, materials, permit fees, water refill, and startup chemicals. They do not include major structural reconstruction if the shell is compromised.

## What Affects the Cost in Boca Raton

### 1. Pool Size

Pool companies charge by the square foot of the surface area. A typical Boca Raton lap pool or backyard oasis ranges from 350 to 600 square feet. At **$18–$25 per sq ft**, a 400 sq ft pool runs roughly **$7,200–$10,000**, while a large 600 sq ft pool pushes **$10,800–$15,000**.

### 2. Material Choice

This is the primary driver of your total budget. Standard white plaster is the cheapest entry point at **$15–$20 per sq ft**. Colored plaster adds roughly **$2–$4 per sq ft**. Aggregate and pebble finishes jump to **$25–$35 per sq ft** due to the cost of stone and specialized labor.

### 3. Pool Shape and Depth

A simple rectangular pool is easy to plaster. Irregular shapes with curves, steps, or tanning ledges require more time and skill. Deep sections also increase labor time because workers must mix and apply plaster at different depths. Complex shapes can add **$1,000–$3,000** to the final quote.

### 4. Structural Repairs

Older pools often have hairline cracks or plumbing leaks that surface during draining. If you discover cracks in the gunite shell, they must be patched before new plaster goes on. Plumbing repairs, like replacing a cracked main drain or skimmer, add **$500–$2,500** depending on the severity.

### 5. Coping and Decking

Resurfacing usually requires inspecting the coping stones around the edge. If the coping is loose or cracked, it needs resetting or replacement. Replacing concrete coping adds **$1,500–$3,000**. Replacing pavers or tile coping can double that cost.

### 6. Water and Chemicals

Boca Raton pools must be filled with potable water. You will pay for the water refill, which can be **$300–$600** depending on the size of the pool. Startup chemicals, including shock, algaecide, and pH balancers, cost another **$200–$400** to prepare the water for use.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| White Plaster | **$15.00 – $20.00** | 7–10 years |
| Colored Plaster | **$18.00 – $23.00** | 7–10 years |
| Quartz Aggregate | **$22.00 – $28.00** | 10–15 years |
| Pebble Tec | **$25.00 – $35.00** | 15–25 years |
| Tile Finish | **$40.00 – $60.00** | 20+ years |
| Full Tile | **$50.00 – $80.00** | 30+ years |

**White plaster** is the industry standard for decades. It is smooth and bright but stains easily from minerals and algae. It is the most budget-friendly option for Boca Raton homes on a tight budget.

**Colored plaster** uses pigments to change the hue of the pool. Common colors are turquoise, blue, or grey. The pigments hide minor staining better than white plaster, but the lifespan remains the same at 7–10 years.

**Quartz aggregate** mixes crushed quartz into the plaster. It creates a harder surface that resists etching from chemicals. This is a popular upgrade in Boca Raton for its durability and subtle sparkle.

**Pebble Tec** uses smooth river pebbles in the finish. It is the most durable common finish available today. It resists stains and lasts twice as long as plaster. Expect to pay a premium for the 15–25 year lifespan.

**Tile finishes** are for luxury applications. A tile band at the waterline looks elegant but does not cover the floor. Full tile covers the entire pool shell and provides a glass-like surface that never stains.

**Full tile** is the most expensive option. It requires specialized installation and removes the porous nature of plaster. It is best for saltwater pools or homes where low maintenance is the priority.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** Palm Beach County enforces the Florida Building Code for all pool alterations. The code requires specific drainage and safety features during resurfacing. If you change the depth or add features, you must submit new engineering plans. The code ensures the pool is structurally sound against soil movement common in South Florida.

**Permits:** Palm Beach County requires a Pool Alteration Permit for resurfacing. You cannot skip this even if you just want new plaster. Apply through the Palm Beach County Building Department portal. Permit fees typically range from **$150 to $400** based on the project valuation.

**Inspections:** Two inspections are standard. The first happens after the shell is prepped but before plaster is applied. The final inspection occurs after the water is filled. The inspector checks for proper drainage, structural integrity, and safety compliance.

**Soil Conditions:** Boca Raton sits on sandy soil with a high water table. Pools are prone to shifting or cracking due to water pressure changes. Resurfacing is a good time to check for structural movement. If the shell has shifted significantly, new plaster will crack within a year without reinforcement.

**Season:** The best time for resurfacing is November through March. Summer heat causes plaster to cure too fast, leading to cracks. Hurricane season (June–November) brings rain that delays draining and refilling. Schedule work during the dry winter months to avoid weather backlogs.

**Insurance:** A resurfaced pool does not directly lower your wind insurance premium like a new roof. However, it improves safety and reduces liability risks. Ensure your contractor carries workers comp and liability insurance. A licensed contractor protects you from claims if a worker is injured on your property.

## How to Save Money on Pool Resurfacing in Boca Raton

1.  **Get at least 4 written quotes.** Contractor pricing varies by **20–30%** for identical scope in Boca Raton. On a **$9,000** job, that is **$1,800–$2,700** in potential savings. Ensure each quote details the material brand, square footage, and permit responsibility.

2.  **Schedule in winter.** November through March is the off-season for pool contractors. You have more leverage to negotiate prices. Contractors are more willing to offer **5–10%** discounts when they need to keep their crews busy.

3.  **Bundle with equipment upgrades.** If your pump or filter is old, replace them during the resurface. Labor is already onsite for plumbing access. Upgrading equipment during this window saves **$500–$1,500** compared to separate projects.

4.  **Choose quartz over pebble.** Quartz aggregate offers most of the durability of pebble at a lower cost. The price difference can be **$3,000–$5,000** on a medium-sized pool. It provides a nice balance of performance and price for most families.

5.  **Check for water rebate programs.** Palm Beach County Water Utilities sometimes offers rebates for water-efficient pool covers or automation. While not for the resurface itself, these programs offset your overall pool ownership costs. Ask your contractor about current local incentives.

## When to Resurface Your Pool — Warning Signs

**Your pool feels rough to the touch.** Old plaster erodes and exposes the aggregate underneath. If your feet hurt when you walk on the bottom, the surface is degrading. This rough texture also harbors bacteria and algae more easily than smooth plaster.

**You see persistent stains.** If you scrub stains and they come back, the plaster is likely porous or damaged. Calcium deposits and metal staining penetrate deep into the plaster over time. Resurfacing removes the damaged layer to reveal a clean surface.

**There are hairline cracks in the shell.** Small cracks are normal, but they can grow into structural leaks. If water is disappearing from the pool faster than evaporation, the shell has failed. Resurfacing seals these cracks and restores the watertight barrier.

**The color is faded or discolored.** Plaster loses its vibrancy after 5–7 years in South Florida. Saltwater pools accelerate this fading. If the water looks grey or green even with proper chemistry, the plaster is compromised.

**Your pool is 10 years old.** Most standard plaster pools last 7–10 years. If you are past that mark, the plaster is likely losing its bond to the gunite. Waiting longer risks structural failure and a much more expensive rebuild.

## How to Hire a Pool Contractor in Boca Raton

1.  **Verify their Florida license.** You need a Certified Pool and Spa Contractor (CSE) or a General Contractor (CGC). Check the license status at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). Do not hire an unlicensed handyman for structural pool work.

2.  **Confirm active insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum **$1 million**) and workers comp. Call the insurer directly to verify the policy is active.

3.  **Check for Palm Beach County registration.** Ensure they have a local business tax receipt. This confirms they are registered locally and not a transient operator.

4.  **Get 3–4 written estimates** with line-item breakdowns — material brand, square footage, tear-off, permits, labor, timeline, and warranty. Compare the scope of work, not just the bottom line.

5.  **Check reviews on Google and Yelp.** Look for patterns in complaints about water refills or plaster curing. A single bad review is normal; three reviews about leaks is a red flag.

6.  **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, drain complete, plaster set, final inspection passed.

7.  **Get everything in writing.** Contract must include: scope, material specs, dates, payment schedule, warranty terms, permit responsibility, and cleanup plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a pool resurface cost in Boca Raton in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$6,000 and $14,000**. The average is around **$9,500**. Your actual cost depends on pool size, material choice, and whether plumbing repairs are needed.

### Do I need a permit to resurface my pool in Palm Beach County?

Yes, always. Apply through the Palm Beach County Building Department. Budget **$150–$400** for the fee. Inspections are required before and after the work begins.

### How long does a pool resurface take in Boca Raton?

The process takes **5–7 days** for an average pool. Draining takes 1 day. Surface prep takes 1 day. Plaster application and curing takes 3–4 days. Refilling and balancing takes 1 day.

### Does a new pool surface increase home value in Boca Raton?

A new pool recoups **50–60%** of its cost at resale. The bigger value is usability — a cracked pool is a liability. A fresh finish makes the backyard marketable to buyers looking for turnkey properties.

### Can I resurface my own pool in Florida?

Florida's homeowner exemption allows some DIY work, but pool resurfacing is highly specialized. You still need permits, inspections, and must meet all safety codes. Insurance companies may refuse to cover a DIY pool resurface.

### Does homeowners insurance cover pool resurfacing?

No. Wear and tear is never covered by homeowners insurance. Insurance covers sudden damage like a tree falling on the pool. Regular maintenance and aging are the homeowner's responsibility.
