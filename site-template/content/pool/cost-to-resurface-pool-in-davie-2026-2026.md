# How Much Does It Cost to Resurface Pool in Davie? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Resurfacing a standard in-ground pool in Davie costs between **$8,500 and $16,500**, with the average homeowner paying around **$12,000**. The final price depends heavily on the surface material you choose, your pool's square footage, and the specific permit requirements of the Town of Davie.

Davie sits in a unique part of South Florida where the water table is high and the soil is often sandy or clay-heavy. This environment puts extra stress on pool shells, making proper resurfacing critical for structural integrity. Ignoring cracks or worn plaster isn't just an aesthetic issue; it can lead to water loss and costly structural failures.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$8,500 – $10,500** | Standard white plaster, 300–400 sq ft |
| Mid-Range | **$10,500 – $13,500** | Quartz aggregate, 400–500 sq ft |
| High-End | **$13,500 – $16,500+** | Pebble or exposed aggregate, 500+ sq ft |

**Budget** options rely on standard white plaster (Marcite). It is the most common choice for budget-conscious homeowners but typically lasts only 7–10 years in South Florida's harsh sun. This price includes draining, cleaning, repairing minor cracks, and replastering.

**Mid-range** upgrades you to quartz aggregate surfaces. These contain colored quartz crystals mixed into the plaster, offering better durability and a wider range of color options. They generally last 10–15 years and resist staining better than plain plaster.

**High-end** covers pebble or exposed aggregate finishes. These are the most durable options, often lasting 20+ years with a natural, textured look. They are more expensive to install but offer the best long-term value for Davie homeowners planning to stay in their homes.

These 2026 estimates cover materials, labor, draining, equipment cleaning, and minor crack repairs. They do not include major structural shell repairs, tile replacement, or new coping stones unless specified.

## What Affects the Cost in Davie

### 1. Pool Size and Depth

Pool resurfacing is priced by the square footage of the water surface and the vertical walls. A typical Davie single-family home pool ranges from 300 to 500 square feet. Larger or deeper pools require more material and labor. A small 300 sq ft pool might cost **$8,500**, while a large 600 sq ft pool with a deep end can push costs past **$15,000**.

### 2. Surface Material Choice

This is the primary driver of your final bill. Standard white plaster is the cheapest at **$25–$35 per sq ft** installed. Quartz aggregate costs **$35–$50 per sq ft**. Pebble finishes run **$45–$65 per sq ft**. The price difference reflects the material cost and the labor intensity required to apply the aggregate textures properly.

### 3. Davie Permit Fees

The Town of Davie requires a building permit for any pool resurfacing project that involves draining the water or structural repairs. Permit fees typically range from **$150 to $400** depending on the assessed value of the work. You must submit a site plan and pool diagram to the Town's Building Division. The permit process ensures the work meets Florida Building Code standards.

### 4. Shell Repairs Needed

Before resurfacing, contractors must inspect the concrete shell. If there are hairline cracks, they are filled with epoxy or hydraulic cement. If the gunite shell has delaminated or has structural cracks, repairs can add **$1,000 to $5,000** to the project. Davie's high water table can cause hydrostatic pressure issues that require weep holes to be installed during the repair process.

### 5. Equipment and Tile Work

If you are also replacing cracked coping, new pool tile, or updating the main drain, costs rise quickly. Tile replacement alone can add **$2,000 to $4,000**. Cleaning and balancing the pool equipment (pump, filter, heater) before the new surface goes on is standard but essential to prevent immediate staining or damage to the new finish.

### 6. Drainage and Disposal

Florida has strict rules about where pool water can go. You cannot drain chlorinated water into storm drains or local canals. You must dechlorinate the water to under 1.0 ppm before draining. This process takes time and may require hiring a specialized disposal service or using a pump to discharge into a sanitary sewer system, adding **$200–$500** to the project.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| White Plaster | **$25 – $35** | 7–10 years |
| Colored Plaster | **$30 – $40** | 8–12 years |
| Quartz Aggregate | **$35 – $50** | 10–15 years |
| Pebble Finish | **$45 – $65** | 20+ years |
| Polished Pebble | **$55 – $75** | 20+ years |
| Glass Bead | **$40 – $55** | 10–12 years |

**White plaster** is the standard option. It is smooth and affordable but stains easily from leaves, tanning oils, and algae. In Davie's humid climate, it requires frequent brushing and chemical balancing to maintain its bright white look.

**Colored plaster** adds pigments to the mix, allowing for blue, grey, or turquoise hues. It costs slightly more than white plaster but offers better UV resistance and hides minor stains better than the standard white mix.

**Quartz aggregate** mixes crushed quartz crystals into the plaster. This creates a harder, more durable surface that resists etching from pool chemicals. The texture is slightly rougher, which is great for foot traffic but can be abrasive to swimwear.

**Pebble finish** uses natural river pebbles or crushed stone for a luxurious, natural look. It is incredibly durable and resistant to algae growth. The rougher texture makes it ideal for families with children who play in the pool, though it requires a slightly higher chemical balance.

**Polished pebble** is a premium version where the pebbles are tumbled to a smoother finish. It offers the durability of pebble with a softer feel underfoot. This is the most expensive option but provides the longest lifespan for South Florida pools.

**Glass bead** surfaces use tiny glass particles for a shimmering, smooth finish. They are very stain-resistant and feel smooth like plaster but are less durable than pebble or quartz. They are a good middle-ground option for those who want a look between plaster and pebble.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** The Florida Building Code (FBC) mandates that all pool resurfacing work must comply with the 8th Edition (2023) requirements. This includes specific standards for shell repairs, the use of approved bonding agents, and the installation of new coping and tile if the existing structure is compromised. The Town of Davie enforces these codes strictly to prevent future structural failures.

**HVHZ and Wind:** While Davie is not in the High Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ) like Miami-Dade or Broward County, it is in Zone 2 or 3. This means pool structures must still withstand high wind loads. If you are replacing pool equipment or adding a new pool cover system, it must meet wind speed ratings of at least 140 mph to satisfy insurance requirements.

**Permits:** The Town of Davie requires a permit for any resurfacing project. You must submit an application to the Town Hall or use their online portal. The process includes a review of your pool's dimensions and a site plan. Permit fees are generally **$150–$400**. Inspections are required after the shell is repaired and before the new surface is applied.

**Insurance Impact:** A new pool surface can improve your home's insurability. Insurance companies in Florida are increasingly scrutinizing pools for safety and structural integrity. A resurfaced pool with updated safety features (like a new main drain cover) can help prevent liability claims. Some carriers may offer a small discount if the pool is updated with modern safety standards.

**HOA Restrictions:** Many Davie neighborhoods have Homeowners Associations with strict covenants. These often dictate the color of the pool finish, the style of coping, and even the type of pool equipment allowed. You must get HOA approval before starting work. Failure to comply can result in fines or being forced to redo the work.

**Season:** The best time to resurface a pool in Davie is during the dry season, typically November through April. The summer months (June–October) bring daily afternoon thunderstorms that can delay drying times and ruin the curing process. High humidity in the summer can also extend the curing time for plaster, leading to a higher risk of surface defects.

**My Safe Florida Home:** If your home is homesteaded and was built before 2008, you may qualify for a free wind inspection. While this program focuses on roof and window upgrades, it can provide insights into overall structural health. There are no specific rebates for pool resurfacing, but general home hardening grants can help offset other costs.

## How to Save Money on Pool Resurfacing in Davie

1. **Get at least 3 detailed quotes.** Contractor pricing varies by **20–30%** for the same scope in Davie. On a **$12,000** job, that's **$2,400–$3,600** in potential savings. Ensure each quote includes a line-item breakdown of materials, labor, permit fees, and disposal costs.

2. **Schedule in the off-season.** November through March is the peak season for pool work in South Florida. Scheduling your project for late autumn or early spring (October or May) might give you leverage to negotiate a **5–10%** discount. Contractors often have more availability during these shoulder months.

3. **Choose quartz over pebble.** If you are on a budget, quartz aggregate offers a significant upgrade over plaster for a fraction of the cost of pebble. The price difference between quartz (**$35–$50/sq ft**) and pebble (**$45–$65/sq ft**) can save you **$2,000–$4,000** on an average pool.

4. **Bundle with equipment upgrades.** If your pump or filter is old, replace it during the resurfacing. Contractors often offer a discount when bundling equipment upgrades with the resurfacing job. Doing both at once saves on labor costs and reduces the need for a second drain cycle.

5. **Do the prep work yourself.** If you are handy, you can clear the pool deck of furniture, plants, and debris before the crew arrives. This can save on labor hours for site preparation. However, do not attempt to drain the pool or repair the shell yourself unless you are licensed.

## When to Resurface Your Pool — Warning Signs

**The surface is rough or crumbling.** If you run your hand along the pool floor and it feels like sandpaper or leaves grit in your feet, the plaster is failing. This is a sign that the bond between the plaster and the gunite is breaking.

**Visible cracks or holes.** Hairline cracks are common, but cracks wider than 1/8 inch or holes that leak water are serious. In Davie's high water table environment, these cracks can allow groundwater to enter the shell, causing structural instability.

**Staining that won't scrub out.** If you have stubborn stains that persist after shocking the pool and scrubbing, the surface is likely porous and broken. This is common in older pools where the plaster has lost its seal.

**Frequent water loss.** If you are adding more than 1 inch of water per week (accounting for evaporation), you likely have a leak. This often indicates the resurfacing has failed or the shell has cracked.

**The pool is 10+ years old.** Standard plaster lasts 7–10 years. Quartz lasts 10–15 years. If your pool is approaching or past this age, it is time to plan for resurfacing. Waiting longer often leads to more expensive structural repairs.

## How to Hire a Pool Contractor in Davie

1. **Verify their license.** Check with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) for a valid license. You need a Certified or Registered Pool Contractor (CPC). Do not hire a general contractor without this specific license.

2. **Check for insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability (minimum $1 million) and workers comp. Call the insurer to verify the policy is active.

3. **Look for local experience.** Choose a contractor who regularly works in Davie and Broward County. They will know the specific permit requirements, water table issues, and HOA restrictions unique to the area.

4. **Get a written contract.** The contract must detail the scope of work, material specifications (brand and type), timeline, payment schedule, and warranty terms. It should also specify who is responsible for permits and disposal.

5. **Check reviews.** Look for recent reviews on Google and Yelp. Pay attention to comments about punctuality, cleanliness, and how the contractor handled unexpected issues.

6. **Never pay 100% upfront.** Florida law limits deposits. A reasonable deposit is **10–20%**. The rest should be paid upon completion and final inspection.

7. **Ask for a warranty.** A reputable contractor should offer a warranty on the new surface, typically **1–5 years** for plaster and **5–10 years** for pebble. Get this in writing.

No, you cannot legally resurface a pool yourself in Florida. The work requires a licensed pool contractor to ensure it meets building codes and safety standards. DIY attempts often void warranties and can lead to structural failures.

### How long does a new pool surface last?

Standard plaster lasts **7–10 years**. Quartz aggregate lasts **10–15 years**. Pebble and exposed aggregate finishes can last **20+ years**. The lifespan depends on water chemistry, usage, and maintenance.
