# How Much Does It Cost to Paint Interior of House in Florida? (2026 Guide)

**Quick Answer:** A full interior paint job in Florida costs between **$4,500 and $12,000** for most single-family homes, with the average homeowner paying around **$7,800**. The biggest factors driving your price are square footage, surface condition, and the humidity-resistant materials required for Florida's climate.

Painting in Florida isn't just about color selection. Your walls face constant humidity, salt air in coastal zones, and intense UV exposure through windows. This requires specific primers and paints that resist mold and mildew, which adds to the material cost compared to drier climates.

## Average Cost Breakdown

| Cost Level | Price Range | Typical Scope |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Budget | **$4,500 – $6,000** | 1,200 sq ft home, 1 coat, basic paint |
| Mid-Range | **$6,000 – $9,500** | 2,000 sq ft home, 2 coats, premium paint |
| High-End | **$9,500 – $15,000+** | 3,000+ sq ft, mold-resistant, full trim |

**Budget** covers painting walls and ceilings in a smaller condo or ranch home. It uses standard latex paint with basic prep like patching small holes. This price often excludes extensive wall repairs or crown molding work.

**Mid-range** is what most Florida homeowners choose. It includes two coats of high-quality paint, detailed trim work, and significant surface preparation like sanding and priming. This handles the typical 2,000 square foot home layout.

**High-end** involves specialized mold-resistant coatings, extensive drywall repair, and premium finishes for cabinets or furniture. It is common in coastal properties where salt air requires higher-grade protection.

These prices reflect Florida market rates as of early 2026. They include materials, labor, basic prep, and cleanup. They do not include major structural repairs if damage is found behind the walls during prep.

## What Affects the Cost in Florida

### 1. Square Footage

Painters typically charge per square foot of wall and ceiling area. A standard 1,500 sq ft home has roughly 6,000 to 8,000 sq ft of paintable surface. At **$3.00–$5.00 per sq ft**, a smaller home runs **$4,500–$8,000**. A 3,000 sq ft home with high ceilings requires more material and labor, pushing costs to **$10,000–$18,000**.

### 2. Surface Condition

Florida's humidity causes drywall to crack and paint to peel faster than in other regions. If your walls have water damage, you need to repair the drywall before painting. This adds **$1,500–$4,000** to the project depending on the extent of the damage. Mold remediation adds even more cost if the mold is active.

### 3. Paint Quality

Standard latex paint costs **$30–$50 per gallon**. Premium mold-resistant paint costs **$60–$90 per gallon**. A 2,000 sq ft interior typically needs 10 to 15 gallons. Using premium paint adds **$300–$600** in material costs but significantly extends the lifespan of the finish in humid conditions.

### 4. Labor Rates

Professional painters in Florida charge **$30–$55 per hour** or **$2.50–$5.00 per sq ft**. Labor is higher in Miami-Dade and Broward County due to cost of living and demand. Rural areas in North Florida might see rates at the lower end of the range, saving you **$1,000–$2,000** on a full job.

### 5. Trim and Doors

Painting walls is cheaper than painting trim. Baseboards, window casings, and doors require detailed brushwork. A home with heavy crown molding and six interior doors will cost **$1,000–$2,500** more than a home with simple trim. Cabinets are often quoted separately at **$150–$300 per door**.

### 6. Location Variance

Prices vary significantly by county. Miami-Dade labor is often **15% higher** than in Tampa or Orlando due to insurance and licensing costs. Broward County requires specific environmental compliance for waste disposal in some cases. Always ask contractors if their quote includes local disposal fees.

## Cost by Material Type

| Material | Cost per Gallon | Lifespan |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Standard Latex | **$30 – $50** | 5–7 years |
| Premium Latex | **$60 – $80** | 10–15 years |
| Mold Resistant | **$70 – $90** | 15+ years |
| Cabinet Enamel | **$80 – $100** | 10+ years |
| Ceiling Flat | **$35 – $55** | 5–7 years |
| Wall Eggshell | **$45 – $70** | 7–10 years |

**Standard latex** is the budget option. It works fine in dry climates but may peel in Florida's humidity. It is best for rental properties or quick flips.

**Premium latex** offers better coverage and durability. It resists scrubbing and mildew better than standard options. This is the recommended choice for primary residences.

**Mold resistant** paint contains antimicrobial agents that inhibit growth in damp areas like bathrooms and basements. It is essential for coastal homes where salt air promotes moisture retention in drywall.

**Cabinet enamel** is a hard-wearing finish designed for kitchen and bathroom cabinets. It resists grease and moisture better than wall paint. Expect to pay more per gallon, but it lasts longer under heavy use.

**Ceiling flat** paint hides imperfections better than sheenier finishes. It is the standard choice for ceilings because it doesn't reflect light. It is cheaper than wall paint but requires the same prep work.

**Wall eggshell** provides a slight sheen that makes walls easier to clean. It is popular in living rooms and hallways. It is slightly more expensive than flat paint but offers better longevity.

## Florida-Specific Considerations

**Building Codes:** Florida Building Code governs interior renovations involving drywall. If you are replacing more than 10% of the drywall surface area in a room, you may need a permit. This is common in coastal zones where water damage is frequent. Check with your local building department before starting major repairs.

**HVHZ:** While painting doesn't fall under High Velocity Hurricane Zone material testing, coastal counties like Miami-Dade have strict air quality rules. Some paints must meet VOC (Volatile Organic Compound) limits. Ensure your paint can is labeled compliant with EPA and Florida standards to avoid fines.

**Permits:** Cosmetic painting usually does not require a permit. However, if painting involves replacing sheetrock or moving walls, a permit is mandatory. Miami-Dade requires permits for most structural changes. Broward County has an online portal for permit applications.

**Insurance Impact:** A fresh coat of paint does not change your insurance premium. However, if you are painting due to storm damage, the claim might cover the prep work. Keep all receipts for materials and labor. Some policies cover interior paint if it was damaged by a covered peril.

**HOA:** Many Florida communities restrict interior paint colors if they affect the exterior view through windows. Some condos require approval for any work that changes the building's appearance. Get written HOA approval before buying materials.

**Season:** Dry season (November to April) is best for painting. High humidity in summer slows drying times and can cause blisters or peeling. Paint dries slower in July, which might extend the timeline by **2–3 days** for a full job.

**My Safe Florida Home:** This program offers grants for exterior hardening. Interior painting is generally not covered unless it is part of a larger mitigation project like mold remediation after a water event. Check eligibility if you have active water damage.

## How to Save Money on Interior Painting in Florida

1. **Do the prep work yourself.** Moving furniture, removing outlet covers, and laying drop cloths can save **$500–$1,000**. Painters often charge for labor even if you do the easy tasks. Clear the rooms completely before they arrive.

2. **Schedule in the dry season.** November through April is slower for painters. You can negotiate better rates or get priority scheduling. Summer backlogs often delay projects, costing you extra in rental housing if you need to vacate rooms.

3. **Choose standard colors over custom.** Custom tinting adds cost to every gallon. Sticking to white, off-white, or beige reduces material waste and cost. If you want a bold color, use it on an accent wall rather than every surface.

4. **Buy your own paint.** Contractors often mark up paint by 20–30%. Buying from a local Florida supplier saves money. Just ensure you buy the exact brand they recommend so it doesn't void their warranty.

5. **Bundle with other rooms.** Painting 5 rooms at once is cheaper than painting 1 room per month. Contractors charge minimums for travel and setup. Combining jobs into one day or week reduces overhead costs.

## When to Paint — Warning Signs

**Your walls look stained or yellowed.** Florida sun fades colors quickly. UV exposure turns white paint yellow over time. If your walls have turned beige, a fresh coat of UV-resistant primer and paint will restore brightness.

**You see peeling or bubbling.** This indicates moisture intrusion. In Florida, this is often caused by humidity or hidden leaks. Do not paint over it. Find the moisture source first, fix the leak, then repaint.

**There is visible mold growth.** Black or green spots on walls mean mold is active. Painting over mold traps it and causes health issues. You need a professional remediation service before any painting begins. This adds **$2,000–$5,000** to the project.

**The paint is older than 10 years.** Most interior paint degrades within a decade. If you are selling your home, fresh paint increases perceived value. Buyers expect a home that looks well-maintained. Old paint suggests deferred maintenance.

**You smell mustiness.** A damp smell often means the drywall is absorbing moisture. If you paint over this, the smell will return. You need to seal the walls with a stain-blocking primer before applying finish coats.

## How to Hire a Painting Contractor in Florida

1. **Verify their Florida license.** Search at [MyFloridaLicense.com](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). You need a Certified or Registered Painting Contractor (CPC or RCP). General contractors can also paint if they have the proper endorsement.

2. **Confirm active insurance.** Ask for a Certificate of Insurance showing general liability of at least **$1 million**. Workers comp is mandatory for any crew of 4 or more. Call the insurer to verify the policy is current.

3. **Check for a local business tax receipt.** Every contractor needs a business tax receipt from the county they work in. This confirms they are registered locally and not a transient operator.

4. **Get 3–4 written estimates.** Compare line-item breakdowns. Look for prep work, number of coats, and brand of paint. A quote without prep details is incomplete.

5. **Check reviews on Google and BBB.** Look for comments about punctuality and cleanup. Florida contractors vary widely in reliability. Consistent complaints about dust or mess are red flags.

6. **Never pay more than 10% upfront.** Florida Statute 489.126 restricts contractor deposits. Payment should tie to milestones: deposit, prep complete, first coat, final walkthrough.

7. **Get everything in writing.** Contract must include: scope, paint brand and color codes, dates, payment schedule, and warranty terms. The warranty should cover peeling and blistering for at least **1 year**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does an interior paint job cost in Florida in 2026?

Most homeowners pay between **$4,500 and $12,000** for a full home. The average is around **$7,800**. Your actual cost depends on square footage, surface condition, and paint quality chosen.

### Do I need a permit to paint my interior in Florida?

Usually no. Cosmetic painting does not require a permit. If you are replacing drywall or moving walls, you likely need one. Check with your local building department before starting major repairs.

### How long does an interior paint job take in Florida?

Walls and ceilings take **2–4 days** for an average home. Cabinets and trim add **2–3 days**. High humidity in summer can add **1–2 days** to drying times between coats.

### Does painting increase home value in Florida?

Fresh paint increases resale value by **1–3%** of home value. It makes the home look cleaner and well-maintained. It is one of the highest ROI improvements for pre-sale staging.

### Can I paint my own home in Florida?

Yes, if you own and occupy the home. You save on labor costs of **$2,000–$5,000**. However, you must handle all prep and cleanup. Mistakes in prep can lead to peeling, which costs more to fix later.

### Does homeowners insurance cover interior painting?

No, cosmetic painting is not covered. Storm damage might be covered if you are replacing drywall and painting due to water damage. Wear and tear is never covered by insurance.
